Thomas v. Nicholas

1. What did the trial judge, HHJ Russen KC, find in relation to the claim in negligence? Was it proven against Mr Thomas or not? What key points were made by the trial judge in coming to the conclusion which he did on the duty of care question?

The claim of negligence was found. The key points were that while it failed in nuisance as a result of being too binding on the land owner there was a duty owed during 'sensitive periods,' by reference to a Canadian case on Mink Breeding Grandel v. Mason. The key element was about public policy and if there was an analogy that could be argued following Robinson. Much concern was raised about the need for the law to not put too great an obligation and since the breeding season was transient and could be worked around in a way to not cause damage then there was a duty of care to act in line with that reasonableness. 

2. The trial judge’s conclusion on duty of care was appealed. Who formed the majority of the Court of Appeal in relation to the question of whether a duty of care was owed by Mr Thomas to the Nicholas brothers; and who was the dissenter?

The Majority formed of Nuggee LJ and Whipple LJ found that the negligence grounds succeeded and therefore appeal dismissed, they also found that the nuisance grounds also succeeded. Moylan LJ found that both the nuisance grounds and the negligance grounds failed and therefore the appeal should be upheld.

3. Which of the majority gave the leading judgment on the duty of care question?

Nuggee LJ. 

4. How was the test of a duty of care, as outlined by Lord Reed in Robinson v CC of West Yorkshire [2018] UKSC 4, analysed by the majority? It will be helpful to tease out the reasoning in a series of steps, to emphasise how the conclusion was reached.

First Nuggee LJ rejects that Caparo is a tripartite test to be applied in every situation by quoting Lord Reed in Robinson stating that the argument should be made by analogy first to other cases and with an eye to maintaining coherence in the law and then if imposing a duty would be fair and just. Therefore the key test from Robinson is that 1. there is an analogous case, 2. applying the analogous case that finds a duty would be coherent in the law, 3. that in the application of the analogous case with a duty the conclusion would be fair and just. 

Second Nuggee LJ approached cases related to neighbourhood. This was not just physical proximity but anyone who could be reasonably harmed by D's actions. 

Third Nuggee LJ found that so long as there was not a total prohibition on taking legal action on one's land then a duty could be imposed justly. In Particular referencing the case of Armistead v. Bowerman. Where a salmon fishery's neighbour was said to not be able to rely on the defense of legal activities when causing harm to his neighbour. 

5. What assistance, if any, did the majority draw from the old Scottish case of Armistead v Bowerman (1888) 15 R 814?

They found it of help to draw the principle that legal activities are not a defense to negligence against the neighbour, so long as there is not a total prohibition and there is a way to act reasonably to avoid harm.

Webster v. Ridgeway Foundation School

1. What were the three particular instances of negligence which HW alleged against the school governors?

At para. 6

"It is said that the school failed to take proper care to keep the site secure. In particular, it is alleged that the school was negligent not to fence the site so as to keep intruders out and negligent in not having a member of staff on duty at the end of the day either on the tennis courts or at the gate between the tennis courts and Inverary Road."

2. What criticisms did Nicol J make of the expert witnesses who prepared reports and who gave evidence in this case? How did the court describe the task of expert witnesses in tort litigation?

At para. 13 and 14

"It is not the role of the witness simply to become an extra advocate for the party which calls him or her."

3. What (if any) duty of care did the school owe to HW? What analogies or distinctions were drawn between HW’s case and other cases in which school children had been injured at or near school premises?

There was a duty of care owed:

The judge examined the Caparo tripartite test and found that there was forseeability of harm for someone coming onto school grounds and harming a student if measures were not taken to avoid that. There is also proximity from a student to a school. However it is argued that it would be wrong, contrary to public policy, to impose a duty in this case. Found that there was a duty ultimately arguing by analogy from road traffic, and that the school had a duty to prevent kids dying on the way to school in a traffic accident on school grounds. Even if done by a third party. So there was a duty even if no assumed duty of care. 

See Para. 121

4. As you progress through the other elements of the tort of negligence in lectures and tutorials, you may find it helpful to refer back to this case, to understand why HW ultimately failed in his claim.

There was no breach since it was based on an ommission.

JOURNAL ARTICLE

1. Why did Mr Haley bring his action in negligence? What heads of damage had he suffered?

He was made deaf following head impact with an explosed pipe during road works which he could not see on account of his condition of blindness. 

2. What was the precedential authority which went against Mr Haley’s claim at the time at which he brought his law suit? What did it hold? How did the House of Lords deal with that precedent?

The Prichard Case where there was an uncovered manhole was to offer him no comfort as it found no liability for a disabled claimant. In the post office case there was a wooden tressle which would have prevented a blind person from falling in as Mr. Haley did. whereas the hammers used for Mr. Haley were far less good in defending him from harm. 

3. In fn 35 of the article, reference is made to the case of Spedding v Dixons Group plc (CA, 23 Oct 1989). (Please note that this case is only available via the Westlaw UK Caselaw database, so it is good practice to find that decision via that database.) Why was Haley distinguished there, and what was decided in Spedding re the duty of care issue?

"However, in my judgment that is quite different from the present case because Haley concerned a flat pavement along which anyone could walk and would see the obstruction but the Board had not in fact taken adequate precautions to protect a person (such as Mr Haley) from falling into what was a trap.

In the present case, the defendants' shop was available for any person who wished to enter and look at an article in which they were interested, which is entirely different from the case of Haley . I therefore do not gain any assistance from that case."

"The defendants in this case were, in my judgment, taking reasonable care to safeguard the plaintiff, so long as she was being accompanied and led by her grand daughter, and the fact that the grand daughter let go of the plaintiff's hand so that the plaintiff moved on her own does not make the defendants liable."

Case about a television shop with a silver strip that was hard to see between the displays of the shop and the regular floor, the claimaint with bad eyesight and having let go of her granddaughter's hand who was guiding her around the shop tripped and fell. Found no liability
