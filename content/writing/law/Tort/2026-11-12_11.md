REMOTENESS

1. The kind or type of damage must be reasonably foreseeable

The law or type of damage was quite narrow.

Joel v. Sutton -> Went up to the HoL on the basis of remoteness. Two teenagers who saw a derelict boat left on a council estate saying, 'rightful owner please claim,' so two teenagers decided to work on it, and jacked it up with a car jack. Then it jolted and crushed one of the teenagers causing extreme disability. Sued on occupier's liability. First instance Trial Judge, said that some personal injury foreseeable from the boat was the damage. CA said falling through rotten floorboards on the deck was foreseeable but falling on the teenager was too remote. HoL went with the trial judge at first instance. Some personal injury from the boat was the type of damage that was foreseeable.

Goldsneider -> Orchestra desireable activity. Practicing in front of the trumbones and then suffered accute hearing loss. Went to CA on remoteness since the kind of hearing loss that results from this is a gradual loss and not accute loss of hearing, that is unusual. So was his hearing loss reasonably forseeable or not. Defined the injury that was forseeable was some injury to hearing. 

Type of damage that is forseeable is to be set wide.

Wizneski v. Central Manchester -> Conceded that the delivery of the baby in question was done negligently. The baby had suffered hypoxia during birth and suffered disability from being strangled by the umbilical cord, and this was very unusual only once or twice a year. The court said damage mechanism did not matter, and that hypoxic brain damage during birth is the point.

2. Eggshell skull rule

Two part test

i. Normal fortitude rule, it must be reasonably foreseeable that a person of normal fortitude would suffer some harm

if true then proceed 

ii. Then D must take C as they lie

Smith v. Leech Brain -> Steel manufacturing firm who had tubs of moltern metal around as an unsafe practice, and when things fell in, then there would be a spray up. The mental illness triggered a fast acting cancer in C's case and it killed him before the claim could be completed. Was this too remote? Held that it was reasonably foreseeable that an ordinary robust claimant would suffer some harm, ie burns, and therefore they were able to recover all the damage as a result of his estate.

3. Even reasonably forseeable damage might not be compensible 

The harm must be within the breach

Samco -> Canary Wharf construction led to lower property values and the security was not enough to cover the mortgage value in that case. Lord Hoffman gave an example. A mountaineer had a full physical before going on the mountain and the doctor misses a dodgy knee, and when the mountaineer is on the mountain suffers brain damage from an avalanche, and the Dr would not be liable because it's a coincidental injury and not related to the risk created by the breach. 

Chester v. Afshar -> Failure to warn liability back pain journalist went to a surgeon and the surgeon suggested spinal surgery and she was not warned of the risk of parapelegia. Duty and breach were met. Causation was also met. 'I would have had to have it, but would have delayed,' majority agreed. Injury that related to her was exactly the risk that would have occured to her. 

Coincidental damage would not be comensible for being too remote. 

There may also be too many causal links 

Hyde v. Thameside -> Mr. Hyde went into hospital with crippling back pain and hypochondriac believed that he was suffering cancer, and became suicidal and then he jumped, hit a lower floor, decided to continue and jumped from the lower floor. Recovered at first instance but on appeal it was too remote because there were too many causal links. Nurses would have to notice, tell doctor, doctor tell psych, and then psych decides to treat or not. Too many causal links in that case. 

DEFENSES

The burden of proof for defenses lies on defendants. 

1. Contributory negligence (CN)

Statutory defense and the source of it is S.1(1) Law Reform (CN) Act 1945

Says where any person suffers in negligence as partly as a result of their own fault, and partly as a result of another's negligence then the court may modify the damages as they believe is just and equitable. 

No need to consider the share it is solely up to the judge. 

Jackson v. Murray -> 13 year old girl who lived on a rural farm, ran out from the school bus and got hit by a car driving at 50 miles, and the driver did not reduce speed, it was dusty conditions. Negligent driving was found, but only 10% share. Appealed, Scottish CA held that there was an error of law and so could intefere, since Judge had said at first instance that the girl was reckless, negligent but not reckless. So changed to 30%. Then SC and they held that it should be 50% because they were equally as culpable, equally blameworthy. 

Negligent D is usually insured but Negligent C is never insured so they just lose their damages. 

Debate if it is a partial or absolute defense. A vicariously liabile D is important.

Collins Wilkinson -> Gunnisbury train station train signalled while the guy was playing getting on and off. Sued on systemic negligent and also vicarious liability. Failed on systemic negligence. Succeeded on single negligence, should have taken further steps. Made a point that any CN found against the man could be taken advantage of for the vicariously liable D. 

Commonly plead

C must be at fault partially, the same things that suppress standard of care for D must be considered for C to see if they were negligent also. Age/Disability etc. 

Point made in Jackson v. Murray -> SoC must be suppressed to that of a reasonable 13 year old. Must take that into account. 

Emergency Circumstances SoC is suppressed based on that. 

Baker -> Where a rescuer has CN found against them their SoC is suppressed. Dr who tried to rescue two workers who went down a well with bad air. Warned not to go down. CA said that there was no CN finding against the doctor who went down there. There was CN found against the workers by contrast. 

2. Must be a causal link between the CN and C's injury

Found in CL and not in statute. Must cause the injury and not the accident


Froom -> Injury suffered exaccerbated by not wearing a seatbelt in a car accident negligently. The CN can contribute to the accident or the injury or both, so there was a finding of CN 20%.

3. The apportionment of CN must be related to the comparative blameworthiness of the two parties

Jackson v. Murray -> The behaviour was equally blameworthy in that case.

Difficult to find a negligence case that finds CN greater than 50% against an employer. 

Neil v. Moore -> Crossing the street with a red man a pedestrian was hit. 20% CN in that case. Greater than that is hard to find because drivers have more power.

The reduction of damages must be just and equitable. 

Unco-operative patients, unlikely to find. 

Smith v. Midstaffordshire -> Smith suffered from circulation issues and was prescribed blood thinners which caused ulcers, but was not taking it as reliably as prescribed. At hospital she was given it but the injury was not referred on leading to gangrene. 

Valenti -> Voluntary assumption of risk, CL so needs a case.

Four elements

Claim that the whole blame for C's injury lies with their shoulder and not D.

Complete defense only. 

ICI v. Shatwell -> Absolute claim

Morris v. Murray -> Case where at a pub crawl two men, and one of them was a pilot and they decided to go up in the plane. C drove to the airfield, bought the fuel and tried to get the propellor going. Then the plane struggled to take off due to adverse weather which was communicated by a NOTAM. Went straight up 100ft, stalled and crashed, pilot died instantly but the co-pilot, claimant survived with bad injuries, tried to claim negligence. But BAC equivalent to 17 whiskey's in the pilot. In a position to know. Appreciation of knowledge is objectively set. 

1. C must be in a position to know and appreciate the risk of injury

Watson -> Attempt to raise Valenti in the context of boxing where a boxer was left with brain damage as a result of negligently not providing ringside medical assistence. D tried to claim Valenti since the aim of boxing is infliction of harm. CA held that Valenti could not be raised again. CA said he appreciated the risk of some injury but not the risk of no medical attention ringside. 

2. C must consent to the specific risk implied

Morris v. Murray -> Consent implied by the actions in buying fuel, driving to airfield and trying to get the prop going. 

3. It cannot have the effect of destroying the content of the duty

Morris v. Murray -> Reasonable care in flying the airplane, no duty to keep the passenger outside of the plane. That duty subsists even if valenti is successful. 

4. Defense must not be barred by law

S.149 Road Traffic Act cannot be alleged in motor vehicle accidents

Baker v. TE Hopkins -> CL barred Valenti for rescuers in that case. Repugnant to public policy 

Very fact dependent.

THERAPEUTIC PRIVLEGE

Only arises in failure to warn. Narrow in application.

Sittaway -> Enables a doctor to withold disclosure of risk if there is a risk of serious psychological detriment to the patient. Controversial defense.

Pierce v. United Bristol -> Defense affirmed in Montgomery but it is not used in that. Experienced mother pregnant and went to see an obstietrician and the baby was late, Dr. said that nature should take its course, and there is a risk of stillbirth when the baby was late, and the risk at that point was 0.1-0.2% and there was no duty to warn because not objectively significant. Reasonable patient test. Highly distressed and anxious woman at presentation and concern that psychological detriment would follow. Rare finding of therapeutic privlege. 

Foregoing the treatment is a harm under therapeutic privlege.
