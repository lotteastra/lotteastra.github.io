In the town of Petersby, several factories dominated the landscape. For 10 years, Anna was employed as an assembly worker at Factory 1, which was owned and operated by Zax plc. Anna was regularly exposed to asbestos dust at that factory. Anna then moved jobs, and worked for five years in Factory 2, which was owned and operated by Yuen plc. She performed the same types of tasks there, and was exposed to asbestos dust at that factory too. Anna fell ill, consulted a physician, and was diagnosed with asbestosis. Statistically, it has been scientifically established that more people suffer from asbestos related illnesses in Petersby than in other areas of the country because of the town’s close proximity to a port, where the transfers of cargos and other activities account for elevated asbestos levels in the general atmosphere. Further, it is impossible to establish, medically, when Anna contracted the illness.


McGhee/Fairchild

1. Only one agent could have caused C's harm and C was exposed to that agent tortitiously and innocently for a reason other than D's breach. 
-> In this case asbestos

2. D failed to take precuations against the risk of the agent causing C harm
-> question of fact

3. D's breach preceeded C's suffering the precise harm which that agent causes
-> Asbestosis causes asbestos related injury

4. C cannot prove that the harm was on the balance of probabilities that his harm was the result of the particular exposure.
-> Impossible to establish when this illness developed. 

5. The highest D can prove is that D's breach materially increased their risk of harm
-> This his the highes A can prove.

A v. Z, Y and companies in the port.

Cannot establish on balance of probabilities which employer or environmental exposure caused her illness. However all would be liable for some proportion of her illness. 

McGhee/Fairchild principle, and also Sinkiwicz v. Grief ltd. 

Therefore causation could likely be proven as these elements could be applied. Liability would be proportional to the years of service with each employer and exposure in this specific environment. Given that S.3 of Compensation act 2006 only applies during melisthemioma cases.


Anna was admitted to hospital as her condition deteriorated. Her husband, Barry, regularly visited her there. Barry was being treated for an eye condition which required that he attend Xatt Clinic to have drops inserted in his eyes under strictly controlled conditions. Barry was told by Wayne, the treating optician employed at the clinic, that he must not expose his eyes to chlorinated water nor to strong sunlight at any time within two hours of the drops being administered. Barry ignored both of these instructions. He regularly drove to visit Anna during the early afternoon when the summer sun was at its strongest, and also frequently swam at a public pool near the hospital, within that two-hour period. Barry’s sight has now deteriorated to the point where he has only 10% vision in each eye. It has come to light that, on two occasions during the treatment programme, Wayne did not administer the drops according to the required protocols. It is impossible to say that this error on Wayne’s part caused Barry’s loss of vision on the balance of probabilities. The highest that the medical evidence will say is that the failure to administer the drops correctly may have caused the loss of vision.
