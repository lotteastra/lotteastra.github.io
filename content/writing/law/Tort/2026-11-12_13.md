Abe planned to go on a mountain hiking tour and was required to undergo a physical examination as part of the “rules of participation”. Abe visited Dr Talik, his general practitioner (GP), at the Elmtree Medical Practice for the physical examination. Dr Talik administered several tests, including an electro- cardiogram (ECG), and certified Abe as being “fit to hike”.

Unfortunately, Dr Talik missed the fact that the ECG showed that Abe had a serious cardiac arrythmia condition. Abe should not have undertaken arduous physical activity until he had received treatment for his condition.

Abe went on the hiking tour and suffered serious injury when strong windy weather caused a large tree branch to fall on the group without warning. Several members of the group were injured as a result, including Abe, who suffered a broken leg.

Advise Abe.

!ANSWER

In this case the damage would be held to be too remote. This is essentially chapter and verse the example given in obiter in Samco. The risk must be within the harm, the risk created by the failure to interpret the ECG correctly would have been a heart attack, or heart issues, but a broken leg from a tree falling would be coincidental and therefore not important. 

Chester v. Afshar -> Failure to warn liability back pain journalist went to a surgeon and the surgeon suggested spinal surgery and she was not warned of the risk of parapelegia. Duty and breach were met. Causation was also met. 'I would have had to have it, but would have delayed,' majority agreed. Injury that related to her was exactly the risk that would have occured to her. 

Please read the recent case of Richardson v William Beech Skip Hire Ltd 2026 EWHC 1341 (KB), delivered on 11 June 2026, and answer the following questions:

1. What degree of CN was found against the claimant Mr Richardson? How was this percentage justified by the court?

!ANSWER
20% 

Statutory defense and the source of it is S.1(1) Law Reform (CN) Act 1945

Says where any person suffers in negligence as partly as a result of their own fault, and partly as a result of another's negligence then the court may modify the damages as they believe is just and equitable. 

So the key finding is that R was at fault for standing so close to the machinery while there was an operator there.

At Para. 70

"In my judgment, sadly the Claimant was careless in looking after his own safety
that day. At 12.40.54 the light on the cab started flashing indicating that Bella
had started the Caterpillar. The Claimant and Mr Gardiner were clearly
expecting the Caterpillar to restart given that they had stepped away from the
machine and were waiting for the Grab to be lowered to read the serial number.
At 12.41.18 the Claimant stepped towards the Grab to remove an item of
dirt/debris from it. That behaviour gives an insight into the complacent mindset
of the Claimant when working around large machinery. In circumstances where
the Claimant must have known that Bella had returned to the cab to start the
Caterpillar, and the light on the cab had started flashing, it was reckless to move
towards and touch the Grab without knowing when it may be activated. That
was not the first time the Claimant had been willing to put himself in close
proximity to the moving Caterpillar that day. Earlier in the scene at 12.33.58,
the Claimant started to take measurements of the Grab whilst the light was still
activated and the Grab had seconds earlier still been rising."

2. ‘Fault’ depends upon deliberate rather than inadvertent acts. Why were Mr Richardson’s act deliberate, according to the court?

!ANSWER
At Para. 74

"The Claimant’s actions were not inadvertent but a
deliberate decision to take the positive action of walking towards the moving
Grab. The action was taken in circumstances where it should have been plain to him (and would have been plain to the reasonably prudent man) that being
between the moving Grab and the machine put him in a position of danger. It
would also have been clear to him that he could have readily waited away from
the Caterpillar for a matter of seconds for the Grab to stop, and for the machine
to be isolated or switched off, before the trying to obtain the serial number."


3. What did the court say about the relative ‘causative potency’ as between claimant and defendant?

!ANSWER
At Para. 77
"The Claimant’s actions, whilst putting himself in harm’s way, do not hold the
same causative potency. Although he was in a position of danger, it was
ultimately the sudden, inadvertent movement of the Grab that caused the
devastating injury."
