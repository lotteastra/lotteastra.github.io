---
title: Tort Law Case Notes Thomas v. Nicholas Duty of Care 1
---

Parties: Claimant: Raptors of Penwith (Nicholas from ROP) Defendant: Upper Cot Estates (Thomas the director of UCE)

Cause of Action: Negligance, this case being on appeal after the judge at first instance found for ROP. Debate on if there was a Duty of Care, and if there was there was an argument by UCE that it had not been breached. 

Duty of Care: Application of Robinson at first instance where it was said that there was a duty not to disrupt the breeding arguing by analogy from that case. 

The claim was eventually established (See para. 104)

Held 2-1 that there was a duty of care. 

Nuggee at Para. 161 

the defendants should not be able to act with complete disregard for the interests of their neighbours in the way they have done."

The Dissenting Judgement however argued that since this area of law covered both nuisance and negligence then there was no gap in the common law to follow and therefore the claim should only be examined in terms of nuisance, applying the Canary Wharf case (Hunter v. Canary Wharf ltd.) The dissenting judge also found that this was a novel case and therefore Robinson didn't apply. 'with a view to maintaining coherence in the law.'

The judge at first instance drew on a case of a Mink Farm in Canada that went to the Supreme Court there, and that negligence liability may be wider than nuisance liability during 'sensitive periods.'
