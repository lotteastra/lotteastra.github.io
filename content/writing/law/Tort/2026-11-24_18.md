Lord Burrows gave a short talk to students in Oxford on 3 June 2025 entitled ‘Omissions in the Tort of Negligence: HXA, YXA and Tindall’. The speech is available at the UKSC website, under the headings, ‘The Justices’, ‘Speeches’, and ‘Lord Burrows’, and at this link:

His concluding words were:

There are, I think, some interesting underlying questions that as students you should be thinking about. I
would mention three:

(i) Is the range of exceptions for failure to confer a benefit sufficiently wide or should new exceptions be developed?

(ii) What precisely is meant by assumption of responsibility? It appears to run from a full-blown contract through non-contractual promises and representations to taking on a task for the claimant with the implicit promise that the task will be performed using reasonable care; and how far is reliance a requirement?

(iii) Is the equality principle - treating individuals and public authorities in the same way - justified OR should the latter have more extensive liability for omissions imposed on them?

Please prepare responses to these questions

Hugo is aged 14 and has special educational needs. He is under the care of Woke County Council which has arranged for him to attend Woke School, a local school operated by the Woke County Council that has special facilities for children with special educational needs.

One afternoon Hugo was working in the school garden. He saw Jerome riding an e-scooter along Puritan Lane, a very quiet lane running past the school. Hugo, who is very thin, managed to slip through a narrow gap in the hedge surrounding the school. He ran in front of Jerome, who had to swerve. Jerome fell off the scooter and banged his head on a wall. Hugo squeezed back into the school. No-one in the school realised that he had been out in the lane.

Kathleen, walking up Puritan Lane, found Jerome a few minutes later at 2.15. She telephoned for an ambulance but by mistake said that Jerome was lying in Cromwell Lane, which is a short distance away. Kathleen said to Jeremy, “You silly fool riding one of those stupid things. Well, I’ve phoned for an ambulance and they say they will be here within five minutes. You should be OK until then and I shall leave you to it.” When the ambulance crew employed by Woke Ambulance Service reached Cromwell Lane at 2.20, they found no-one in need of their services. They decided that it must have been a false alarm and drove slowly back to the station stopping to buy some food on the way and arrived at 2.45.

The controller at Woke Ambulance Station was, however, somewhat concerned and at once rang Kathleen on her mobile phone. Kathleen however had now switched her phone off and did not answer the call.

At 2.55 another passerby on Puritan Lane found Jerome unconscious. She rang for an ambulance, and another ambulance from Woke Ambulance Service arrived at 3.00. Jerome has suffered serious injuries and will have permanent disabilities. There is a strong chance that, if Jerome had been treated by the paramedics by 2.40 at the latest, he would have suffered much less serious disabilities.
