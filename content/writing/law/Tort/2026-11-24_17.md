title: Tort Law Negligence by public bodies
---

For a long time there was no special subject of public authority liability and a new invention. In English law AV Dicey, English law does not distinguish so hard between private and public bodies and they are subject to the same basic laws. The circumstances may be different but are accessible to all government defendants.

Public bodies started to acquire certain duties and new powers. They can also be liable if they carelessly use their powers and degredate their duties. They will be liable in the law of tort for negligence.

Litigants tried to take advantage of the relaxing of duties. Caparo as an example of that. Duty of care and local councils may fail to deal with this issues. However only in exceptional circumstances does the duty be able to be brought by an individual. There must be something extra. A lot of those litigants failed.

Start from the proposition that such cases are unlikely to suceed when the defendant is a public body and see if you can track down that something extra.

There are three ways in which these cases come before the court.

1. Action directly against the public authority or through vicarious liability. Not much difference. 

2. Cases where the individual who is injured is suing a private company and that company is trying to join a public authority into the private company's business and apportion some kind of liability.

3. Many of the cases are striking out actions. A defendant, a local council, is saying that even if everything is proved, there is no duty of care. So the action is about if the case should be struck out for lack of duty and this avoids a lengthy trial. 

Some policy issues. 

1. Whether there tort should be the action, there are other remedies, letter to the ombudsman, judicial review, human rights act cases. 

East Suffolk River Catchment Board v. Kent -> The catchment board was a public authority and tried to act when someone's land was flooded and they screwed it up, but they didn't cause any extra harm. They just failed to get rid of it. There was no duty owed to be successful in removing the flood water. There was no 'something extra.'

2. Is it right that public funds should be used to compensate individuals? 

2023 Case against two councils -> Failed to use powers to protect vulnerable youths, but it was struck out, there were about 1,000 cases and if they were not struck out, the council might have a long and extensive trial. The insurance companies themselves might increase their cover and that might be unjust. 

3. In many cases the public authority has to balance competing issues and conflicting interests.

Prisoners -> They should be able to be released into the public area, however they might if they are released then create greater harms, and the question of harms when councils allow a prisoner to stay home with their kids to see if something happens. 

Preliminary matters for tort public authorities

First many of the cases involve ommissions. Robinson case, difference between causing harm, or did they fail to make things better. It is more difficult to establish liability if they fail to make things better.

Conflicting policies. 

Third parties, the defendant failed to prevent and detain a third party. Thomson. 

Policy distinction as well.

Content of duty of care for public authorities

Failure to use powers or to use them effectively

Stovin v. Wise -> Man injured when on a motorbike when someone merged across. The defendant tried to bring in the council because they were obstructing line of sight. HoL held that they did not owe a duty of care in that case. They had appreciated the danger, but that was not enough to be something extra. 3-2 decision. 

Gorringe v. Calderdale -> Driving up a hill over the brow of the hill couldn't see over, there was a bus that appeared to be on the driver's side of the road, and took unnecessary evasive action and led to injury. Asked for signs to be put up, but that was not enough. Failure to use powers effective was not something extra. 

Positive Action by a public authority creating danger may lead to liability

Knightley v. Johns -> Example of that. 

Kane v. New Forest DC -> Busy main road with no crossing but the council built a housing estate nearby and they kept on trying to cross the road, held that there was a positive action leading to danger. 

Jain v. Trent Strategic Health Authority -> HoL expressed regret at the outcome they reached. Statutory responsible for nursing homes. Nursing home was taken away. Statutory responsibility for the wellbeing of the residents of that care home not present. Regrettable outcome. Duty was to the residents of the area and not the care home.

Tofaris and Steel -> Analysis of the liability of public authorities. Arguing for greater liability for public authorities. They did not support the conclusion and have been increasingly narrow as to what something extra may be. 

Assumption of responsibility

Tindall -> Assumption of responsibility involves the idea that a person may by words or conduct expressly or impliededly promise (or undertake or give an assurance) to take care to protect another from harm.

Darnley v. Croydon Health Services -> Undertaking to provide services is an assumption of responsibility. Darnley attacked in the street and wait for hours. No question of something extra in that case, that was sufficient. 

Health service is a bit exceptional

Kent v. Griffiths -> Assured that an ambulence is on its way and it created harm through delayed treatment. Assumed responsibility creating a duty of care. 

Barnett v. Enfield -> Assumed a duty over a child when they took a child into their care. They had assumed responsibility over the child.

Phelps v. Hillingdon -> Special needs child and liable for the failure of the educational psychology for the needs of the child. They had assumed an extra duty. A specific obligation towards a specific identified individual. 

Unsuccessful examples - No assumptions of responsibility

Michael v. South Wales Police -> Wrongly downgrading the severity of a call about a woman who her boyfriend was coming to get to her. The call handler did not give a specific assurance in Kent, and the police are different from the health service. 

Furnell v. Flaherty -> Outbreak of a disease and children became ill. Tried to bring in health prevention authority and that was not enough for the health protection authority to assume responsibility. 

Tindall -> Mr. Kendall lost control of car on black ice and they put up a sign, and then people slid on the black ice. Argued that the police had assumed responsibility by putting up a sign. Nothing that anyone else could rely on through that sign that they took down. 

Chief Constable of Northhamptonhire Police v. Woodcock -> Miss Woodcock had been in an on off relationship with there being damage to property and issues. Advised to keep room locked. However man was hanging out around, and attacked when she went off to work. No assurance or assumption that they would call and look after her. Fact sensitive. 

Sintes v. London Borough of Tower Hamlets -> General summary of the law at paras 63-79. Claimant was injured when tripping over a pole carelessly placed by market traders at the market stalls outside Whitechapel Station. There was not anything extra. Closer to Tindall. They had authorized a stall but they had not assumed responsibility for her specifically. 

Suresh v. GMC -> Anaesthetist with an unblemished record who recieved a letter from the GMC opening an investigation into his fitness to practice, leading to his suicide. Should've taken reasonable care in how the news was broached. CA argued that this could not be argued. The duty was owed to patients and not the doctors as employees. 

Social Work cases

Social services departments within councils with abused/neglected children. Assumption of responsibility is the only basis of this. Earlier it was Caparo but now it is assumption of responsibility.

Neglected or abused children

X v. Bedfordshire County Council -> Hard to follow case. Stretched the intellectual powers of undergraduates arguably. What was said is a starting point. Social workers did nothing despite a lot of reports. No assumption of responsibility in that case.

M v. Newham -> Child was removed from the mother. Abused by someone 'said John,' placed with foster parents and the mother was allowed to listen but it was a different John and returned to the mother, and affected by this year. There was an assumption in that.

JD v. East Berkshire, MAK v. Dewsbury, RK and AK v. Oldham -> Three cases only in Dewsbury that the children brought, all the others were parents brought in addition to the child in that case. In Dewsbury bruising, but it was as a result of disease and not abuse, and the CA in that case said that there was not stirking out. ECHR, child did not appeal against the ruling, the parents did. Left with a CA authority, Lord Bingham said that the CA should have done what they did, and struck it out. 

N v. Poole -> Council accepted responsibility and provided a home, the home was adjacent to a known antisocial person who subjected the disabled people who were living there. Sued for the psychological harm over the years. Council was sued under vicarious liability but unsuccessful. Dewsbury was inconsistent with X v. Bedfords hire, but the SC held that the reasoning was incorrect but reached same conclusion. Judgement from para 66. No assumption on the facts. Not assumed responsibility to protect from a third party.

HXA v. Surrey County Council, YXA v. Wolverhampton City Council -> Had meetings where they talked about it but didn't take any action. But there was a series of sexual offences established against the person who they talked about. The other case was an eplieptic and taken away from parents for a while because the child was difficult to care for. Sense that they did assume responsibility for the respite care but that was not the allegation it was about a different issue. Paragraph 86 explains why there is no duty of care. Paragraph 106 where it can arise, two kinds of cases, where the council has taken over care of a child as in Barrett v. Enfield there is an assumed duty of care, assumption during the respite care. No responsibility after the child was sent back. Assumption of responsibliity was the only basis that the council could be held liable, means assuming responsibility for the particular child. 

Poole -> Waste of time and money to investigate cases like that. 

JD v. East Berkshire -> Took their case as a wrongly accused parent to the HoL, and it found that the claim should be struck, Bingham dissenting, on the basis of policy reasons. Conflict of interests, the interests of parent and child are in conflict the same as Jain and the duty was to the child and not to the parents if the council acts in a way. 

Making matters worse 

Council makes matters worse than they would have done otherwise. 

Kent v. Griffiths -> The ambulence case, where they made things worse, without that assurance then things would have gone better. 

Tindall -> Similar argument as Kent but that failed in that case. Paragraph 58 in that case. Not enough to show that the actions made things worse but it needs to be foreseeable that they would have made something worse. 

Woodcock -> Failed, if the call handler had given a different answer there was no indication of anything.
