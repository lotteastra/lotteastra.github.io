OCCUPIERS LIABILITY

Most frequently sued D

Where there are entrants who are injured on entry to premesis. Covers employees and people injured in motor vehicles. Occupier and premesis is a wide definition but it generally is found to be narrow. 

Divides between visitors and trespassers. 

Occupier's Liability Act 1957 -> Visitors

Occupier's Liability Act 1984 -> Trespassers

Negligence is the same elements the acts intervene for duty and breach but not causation and remoteness and those are solely common law. Defenses are shared competence.

Three prerequisites to establish OLA claim, if any of them claim then it is ordinary action in negligence.

1. D must be an occupier

2. Where accident happened was a premesis

3. The harm must be caused by a static danger

D MUST BE AN OCCUPIER

Who is an occupier

Wheat v. Laken -> Wherever a person has a sufficient degree of control over a premesis such that they could realise that someone coming onto the premesis there could be some harm. Wide ranging area. 

Wheat v. Laken -> Pub case brewery owned a pub on three levels, pub on the ground floor, on the first floor the publicans lived there and the second floor was furnished for summer guests. The brewery retained ownership and had a right to inspect and repair the premesis. C went down a staircase to get a drink from the bar, they didn't come back, pitch black due to no lightbulb in the staircase, fell down the stairs. Laken was an occupier on account of the reserved right rendering some degree of control due to repair and inspections. The publicans were also occupiers because they furnished the rooms and set the rules. There was no breach in the end. The missing light was not theirs to find.

Premesis found in the act S.1(3)(a) premesis includes any fixed or moveable structure, a plane, vessel or car. 

Can arise to buildings the condition of the land, a ladder etc. Fixed or moveable structures. 

Premesis must be dangerous and defective

Must be out of repair or have a latent danger or haven't operated as intended. 

Ruth Gerie -> Wetherspoons case with the bannister and sailed down the staircase broke her back after falling down. Court held that the premesis were dangerous but they were not defective and therefore it had to go under ordinary negligence.

What the injury came from must be the static condition of the premesis. The wording would not imply this but ultimately the courts have said that it only relates to static conditions and activities on premesis must be different.
