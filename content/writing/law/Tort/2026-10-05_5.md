With pure ommissions the rule is that English law does not generally impose a duty of care. The bad samaritan principle is the best example of that. With respect to the next part, most things are not pure ommissions but a combination of both. A distracted driver on their phone and they hit the car in front of them. Mr. Bird was a deliveryman and he was supposed to leave his horse drawn van tethered going to get a reciept. Topp v. London Country Bus

Arose because the bus company had a lax system of changing drivers in between, and they would leave the bus not secured and the next driver would go there. During 9 years a person unknown broke into the bus and drove it away after it was left for 9 hours. Then the driver hit a cyclist who died after an injury FAA. Was a duty of care owed by the bus company to her. It smelt like a pure omission, the CA held that this was a pure omission and Haynes could not be established. The danger in Haynes was inherent but a bus is not inherently dangerous. Human intervention made it dangerous and it was distinguishable.
