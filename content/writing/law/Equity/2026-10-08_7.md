The Settlor intended to benefit someone for a trust. Mandatory because there is an obligation to benefit the beneficiary. 

Certainty of object, which is a person, an identifiable beneificary or group of beneficiaries, it cannot be for a chattle, but only a person. Is it sufficiently clear who the beneficiaries are. 

The different tests depend on the trust. 

Conceptual certainty - Is the class of beneficiaries sufficiently clear

Evidential certainty - Can the objects prove that they belong to that class

Ascertainability - Can the beneficiaries be located

Size of class/Administrative unworkability - Is the class too big to be workable 

Capriciousness - The class itself makes no sense, it is arbitrary

Fixed trusts -> Fixed trust, the trustee has no choice on what they give the beneficiary. There is no discretion on the distribution for the trustee. 

Discretionary trusts -> Trustee has some discretion on how much to give and to whom. 

Powers -> The power holder has no obligation to distribute at all. 

Fixed trust - Need conceptual and evidential. Conceptual certainty achieved by a complete list test. The trustee must be able to produce a complete list of all beneificiaries, at the time of distribution but not at creation. Objects must be able to prove with evidence that they come within the class. Evidential and conceptual certainty is necessary.

No issues regarding ascertainability, administrative unworkability or capriciousness, because those parts of it are unnecessary to consider. 

IRC v. Brodway Cottages Trust -> "A trust for such members of a given class of objects as the trustees shall select is void for uncertainty, unless the whole range of objects is at least capable of ascertainment." 

Condition precedent -> Distribution subject to a condition being met. If one person can satisfy the condition then that will be sufficient. 

Re Barlow's Wills Trust -> Testatrix left some paintings for sale but said that family or friends could purchase them at a lower rate, it is not necessary that everyone needs to be identified so long as one person can be identified then that is sufficiently certain. The object shows that they belong to the class. 

Condition subsequent however is stricter. For example where there is a trust created that will pay out following some event coming to pass, then there needs to be evidence both of being part of this class, and the subsequent condition being met. 

Re Jones -> Beneficiary forfieted property if she had a social or other relationship with named person, that failed because of lack of certainty for that condition. It wasn't possible to work it out. 

Discretionary Trusts -> Trustee has discretion

Initially the complete list test was applicable. However this is difficult.

Is or is not test 

Fixed trust, complete list, Re Broadway Cottages

Discretionary trust, is or is not test

MCHPAIL v. DOULTON

Mr Baden creates a trust for the benefit of 'any officers, employees, or ex-officers or ex-employees or any relatives or dependants of any such person.' 

Lord Wilberforce at 221 

"The conclusion which i would reach is that the wide distinction between the validity test for powers and that for trust powers, is unfortunate and wrong and that the rule recently fastened on the courts by the Broadway Cottages case ought to be discarded, and that the test for validity of trust powers ought to be similar to that accepted by this house in Re Gulbenkian's Settlements Trusts for powers namely that the trust is valid if it can be said with certainty that any given individual is or is not a member of the class." 

Conceptual certainty - In a discretionary trust this is the is/is not test

Evidential certainty - Follows Re Boden Deeds no. 2

Ascertainability - will not invalidate the trust, see Emery article

Administrative unworkability - Wilberforce suggested that a DT to benefit all residents of Greater London would fail for that reason in R v. District Audtor ex parte West Yorkshire MBC 

Re Baden no. 2 CA applies the test -> is/is not test, relatives or dependants was this conceptually certainy. Judges agreed it was conceptually certainy but they had different reasonings. 

Sachs LJ - is/is not is concerned with coneptual certainty only and evidential certainty not important. If a person could not prove they were a member of the class then they would fall outside but the trust itself would still be valid so long as one class could be ascertained. 

Megaw LJ - Enough so long as it can be shown that a substantial number of objects come within the class

Stamp LJ - It had to be shown that of any given person that they either were or were not within the class, that does not mean a complete list but if there is uncertainty about any one person the trust fails. If there is uncertainty of one object then that would destroy the whole trust. 

Discretionary trusts are bigger. The trustee has to suvey the class in a DT.

Caprciousness is also an issue but there is no case were that alone defeated finding a trust. 

Re Hays ST -> "Must not simly proceed to excercise the power in favour of such of the objects as happen to be at hand." "He must consider what persons or classes of person are objects within the definition." "No need to complie a complete list or even make accurate assesment of the number." "What is needed is an appreciation of the wdith of the field." "Only when the trustee has applied his mind to the size of the problem should he consider individual cases." 

The survey must be done first before discretion can be used.

Re Manisty's Settlement -> Power to benefit residents of Greater London is capricious because the terms of the power negative any sensible intention on the part of the settlor.

Re Hays -> In recognizing the validity of intermediate powers he did not consider the size of the class would prevent trustees from excercising their duty. 

Trustee arbiter -> Can decide who falls within or not. They cannot resolve conceptual uncertainty, is too much delegation, the settlor/testator must define it conceptually. 

Arbiters may exist for evidential certainty. 

Trustee arbiter cannot decide who is the class. 

A third party Arbiter, cannot resolve conceptual uncertainty.

Re Tucks Settlement -> Chief Rabbi 'approved wife of Jewish Blood,' so it is conceptually certain and they just determine the evidence. Lord Denning went further but was in the minority. 

Perpetuity periods 

Rules agains remoteness of vesting

Must be allocated to a person within a reasonable period of time. Life in being plus 21 years, or period of 125 years. Trust cannot go longer than 125 years. 

Perpetuity period in common law is life plus 21 years, or statutory 125 years. 

Go step by step

Certainty of Intention, did they intend to create a trust, power or gift.

Certainty of Subject Matter, can the subject matter be identified.

Certainty of Object, can the beneficiary be identified, is it discretionary or fixed, and that will govern it.
