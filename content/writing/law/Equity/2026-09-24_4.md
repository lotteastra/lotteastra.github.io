Farepak Foods and Gifts v. Revenue and Customs

Facts: Farepak was a company that allowed customers to pay in money throughout the year which would then be used to pay for christmas gifts at the end of the year. This service then went into administration with Mann J commenting that the business was 'heavily insolvent,' and the returns could be expected to be 'pennies on the pound.'

The company operated by having the customers pay agents who were often but not always known to the customer personally, the agents then passed the funds over to the company itself which then bought and distributed the christmas goods for the customers.

Legal question: Could a trust be construed from this set of transactions so as to prioritize customers over other creditors?

There were three grounds for this. 

Firstly a Quistclose trust where a creditor has lent money for a particular purpose it is taken to be held on trust, and therefore it can be traced and returned. This was not found in this case as the money was mixed together and for an unclear purpose. The mixing of the accounts proved fatal in the implication of a trust in this manner and it therefore failed. 

Secondly, a constructive trust over the monies paid after insolvency proceedings began. However this failed on account of the particulars of the business arrangement too. In particular that agents recieved money and then may have taken time to send that money onto the company, and equally that the monies took time to reach the actual account and therefore a trust could not be constructed.

Thirdly, an express trust over the monies paid after insolvency proceedings began. There was a trust deed executed to prevent money going into the account recieved from customers. However the deed was not properly executed. It listed the wrong account than the one that actually recieved money from agents. This deed was rectified, however the practical difficulties in finding the customers that arose from this particular business model acted to prevent this express trust from taking effect. The beneficiaries who would have benefitted from this express trust were to uncertain to give effect to it.
