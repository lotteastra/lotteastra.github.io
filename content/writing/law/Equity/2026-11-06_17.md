Harpreet made the following dispositions in her validly executed will:

(a) “£500,000 to my sister, Jatinder, trusting that she will use a fair share to maintain our much-loved aunt Talwar during her lifetime. The remaining amount to be divided between my surviving nieces and nephews in such proportions as Jatinder, in her absolute discretion, thinks fit.”





(b) “My prized collection of ten vintage motor cars (which was valued at £800,000 in 2023) to be held on trust by my good friend, Greta. Greta must auction the cars and transfer the proceeds of sale to the ‘Slow-Horses Vintage Automobile Club’ within a year of my death. The Club shall then use the money advanced for the purpose of buying and/or maintaining suitable cars that qualify to participate in the annual ‘London to Brighton Veteran Car Race’. The Club must enter this race at least once within five years of receiving my legacy.”





(c) “£25,000 to the Llaregyb Guild of Lawyers to be used for the purposes of financing their annual dinner for the next five years and for awarding a prize at each event to the most promising new member of the Guild.”

Harpreet died in August 2024. Advise Liz and River, the trustees of Harpreet’s estate, as to the validity of the above dispositions. They are anxious to carry out the terms of Harpeet’s will, but they mentioned to you the following in a meeting you had with them last week: 

(i) Jatinder has informed Liz and River that Talwar died last month. Jatinder would like to use the £500,000 to buy a small cottage in the Cotswolds for herself as she feels that Talwar would have wanted her to enjoy life while she still can. In any event, she is troubled by the fact that she doesn’t know the whereabouts of all Harpreet’s nieces and nephews despite having undertaken a thorough search on Facebook.  

 



(ii) Greta has told the trustees she thinks that auctioning Harpreet’s old cars is a good idea, but, in her opinion, giving the proceeds of sale to the Slow-Horses Club makes no sense and is a complete waste of money. Apparently, Greta read in the local newspaper that this private members Club is in a dire financial situation. She would prefer to give the money to an organisation that seeks to address the climate emergency instead. Gilly, the president of the Slow-Horses Club, has also contacted Liz and River to say that the Club would be prepared to honour Harpreet’s wishes, but it would like to use half of any money advanced to pay off the Club’s bank overdraft.





(iii)      Liz and River told you that the Llaregyb Guild of Lawyers is in decline. In fact, it only has two surviving members (a president and a treasurer). They had already decided to dissolve the organisation before hearing of the bequest and now they are firmly set on this course of action. The trustees are worried that the two remaining members might be tempted to keep the £25,000 for themselves.
