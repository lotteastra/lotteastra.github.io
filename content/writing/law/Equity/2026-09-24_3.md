Equity developed in paralell to the Common Law in order to address the failings of that system, in particular percieved rigidity with regard to the drafting of writs. 

Equity was fused with the Common Law system in 1873 BUT Equity still exists as a seperate doctrine within the greater Common Law system. It exists to furnish but not replace. Though as noted in that 1873 Act in a conflict between the two Equity prevails. 

Equity and Common Law can act cocurrently on the same subject matter. An example would be recisission of contract wherein Common Law has fewer reasons for recisission than Equity and therefore Equitable remedies are more forgiving and attractive. 

Equity also has remedies like injunctions and in particular estoppel. Estoppel is significant as it demonstrates the differences between Common Law and Equity, this is because it exists for a situation where a contract was malformed and otherwise wouldn't be recognized, but it would be unconscionable not to recognize it, perhaps as a result of detrimental reliance. Therefore equity operates where the common law would or could not. 

Equity also places an emphasis on relationships in particular fiduciary relationships, where one is supposed to act in a position of trust on behalf of a beneficiary. This allows for a kind of dual ownership which allows another party's rights to exist and be enforceable where in Common Law they would not be. 

There are debates on if Equity should be allowed to continue to exist seperately or if it should be fully assimilated into Common Law. PROFESSOR BIRKS argues that it should be assimilated as part of how he analyzes private law more generally with disputes falling into one of four buckets. Consent, wrongs, unjust enrichment and other events. Given that equity does not fit in any of these one buckets then it should be assimilated he argues. Though this view is far from universal. Lord Millet argues that because other historical legal systems have themselves had two parts then Equity remains part of that. 


Equity also has multiple kinds of trust, there is a bare trust confers a strong equitable right to withdraw as the beneficiary wishes with the trustee just managing. A fixed express trust is where the beneficiary cannot direct the trustee on how to manage the money though all beneficiaries enjoy the same right. A discretionary trust confers the weakest right because it can only be excercized at the discretion of the trustee with property only being given out as they see fit.
