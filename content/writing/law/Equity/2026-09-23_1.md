Equity is a supplement to the common law. It fits in with the rest of the system and you need to conceptualize it within the rest of the system. It involves conscience, and gives extra remedies that you don't have at common law, as well as protecting specific intentions and morality. The courts also act to form a balance between statutes and equity. Conscience is integral to equity. Every trustee is a fiduciary and they owe duties of trust as part of that relationship which are recognized in equity. 

Beneficiary gives the trustee property with terms, where they are the legal owner but their ownership is subject to restrictions based on that relationship. A conscience relationship between the beneficiary and a trustee. This relationship translates to a property right for that beneficiary, an equitable property right, an equitable interest which allows them to hold the trustee to account. 

You hold property with respect to someone else's interests which are enforceable against you based in equity. This might mean that the flexibility becomes strict, the common law rights are more flexible but they are strict to protect the beneificary from the trustee as part of this fiduciary relationship. Settlor gives the property to the trustee, for a beneficiary. In vivos trust for the living. Settlor in vivos trust, testator trust created in a will. You can't walk back a trust once you've created it. 

This is a private law conception of conscience, that the owner is required to use the property for the purposes that it was vested in them. Think of the crusader and the templar and the origin of equity in that respect. Trust property they give the property to the trustee, subject to obligations and then the beneficiary recieves entitlements in return. There are also constructive trusts. 

One property but two owners with different rights over it. 

Gift/Trust. If there is someone who has the property who would be better placed to keep hold of the property while still passing on the benefit to the beneficiary who you intend the gift for. The benefit is determined through context. You can also set rules, parameters and obligations. You can also maintain an interest in a trust, but you can't create a trust for yourself. 

There are also resultant, implied and constructive trusts. Resultant trusts can come from a screw up. Constructive come from unconscionable situations. 

Three certainties are essential for an express trust. These will be assessed absolutely.

Certainty of Intention -> Intention to create a trust
Certainty of subject matter -> Related to exact property
Certainty of Object -> With a specific beneficiary
