Tutorial Questions

1. Why does a trust need a beneficiary like a car needs an engine?

Equity creates rights inter personam and not inter rem, which in turn means that without someone to enforce those rights then those rights cease to function. In the same way a car without an engine ceases to function. 

2. Should the courts continue to recognise “concessions to human weakness or sentiment” as an exception to the beneficiary principle?

No. Because there's no mechanism of enforcement then a trust without a beneficiary simply cannot work. 

3. What are the ways of giving effect to a gift of property to an unincorporated association? What are their relative strengths and weaknesses?

There is the absolute gift, which is weak because members can sever their share and leave which means there is no guarantee whatsoever of the funds being used in a way related to the purpose for which they were given. 

There is also the Contract Holding theory, where as part of each member's contract with the UA they will hold the asset but it is not severable. This is preferrable to an absolute gift because an absolute gift would have no chance, this has some chance. 

4. Barbara died last month. Her validly executed will contained the following dispositions: 

(a)  ‘I despise my living relatives and have no close friends. I wish to leave £500,000 to ensure that my giant tortoise, “Galapagos”, and my pack of hounds are properly cared for after my death’

This is enforceable even though the purpose is to benefit chattels, this is recognized under the Re Endracot limited exceptions. Re Deam and Pettingal v. Pettingal care of horses in the latter case, lines up close enough so as to create a trust. 

(b)  ‘I hereby bequeath £100,000 to further the good deeds of the Marvel League (UK branch).’

This would not be able to exist as a trust, unless the Marvel League was incorporated as a corporation in which case the corporation could as a seperate legal person be the beneficiary of the trust. 

If it was an unincorporated association then it could be a gift to the members but held as part of their contractual relationshipss to each other. They would be able to make the captain America structure if permissible. 

Though Re Grant -> Labour Party the gift could not be enforced because the people given it do not have control of the organization and there is another organization towering over it.

(c)   ‘I leave £25,000 to the Student Film Society at the London College of Knowledge (where I studied the performing arts). The Society must use the money to hold a truly grand film festival at a topnotch venue in memory of me.’

No obligation to do anything following Re Lipinski. However the money would be gifted to the association and held insofar as they have a contract with everyone. They can wind up the society and ignore the wishes perfectly fine as this is a gift and not a trust.
