Tutorial Questions

1. Why does a trust need a beneficiary like a car needs an engine?

2. Should the courts continue to recognise “concessions to human weakness or sentiment” as an exception to the beneficiary principle?

3. What are the ways of giving effect to a gift of property to an unincorporated association? What are their relative strengths and weaknesses?

4. Barbara died last month. Her validly executed will contained the following dispositions: 

(a)  ‘I despise my living relatives and have no close friends. I wish to leave £500,000 to ensure that my giant tortoise, “Galapagos”, and my pack of hounds are properly cared for after my death’

(b)  ‘I hereby bequeath £100,000 to further the good deeds of the Marvel League (UK branch).’

(c)   ‘I leave £25,000 to the Student Film Society at the London College of Knowledge (where I studied the performing arts). The Society must use the money to hold a truly grand film festival at a topnotch venue in memory of me.’



Galapagos is only five years old and Barbara owned 15 dogs. Dave (Barbara’s nearest living relative) has told you that he reluctantly made a promise to look after the animals for her, if the need arose. However, he has disclosed to you that he would rather keep the money for himself if he had a choice.



The Marvel League (UK branch) is a private organisation, which exists to promote and honour the heroic characters portrayed in Marvel Comics (which include the X-Men, the Hulk, Iron Man and Captain America). It has 500 members, and it is currently short of funds. It would like to construct a bronze sculpture to celebrate the awesome bravery of Captain America if that was legally permissible.

The Student Film Society at London University is not affiliated to the National Students Union. On hearing of Barbara’s passing, the Society’s president and treasurer (the only elected officers) propose to wind up the Society. It has 50 fully paid-up members and it currently has funds of £500.

Advise Dave (the Executor of  Barbara's will) as to the validity of each of the above dispositions.
