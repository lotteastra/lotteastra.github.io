Tutorial Questions
 
1. What is meant by a 'gratuitous transfer'? Name three modes by which such a transfer can be accomplished.

2. Why, in Milroy v Lord, did Lord Turner refuse to contemplate construing an imperfect gift as a completely constituted trust? 
 
3. Diana owned three houses (in fee simple). She wanted to give one house (‘The Cottage’) to her daughter, Alice, and another house (‘The Villa’) to her son, Robert. Diana executed the forms to be used to transfer the two properties to Alice and Robert respectively. She then left on a round the world adventure holiday. 

On the day she left, Diana delivered both transfer forms to Alice and asked her to take care of the registration to complete the transfers to both properties. Alice agreed to perform these tasks; however, as a result of a personal crisis, she forgot to submit the forms for registration. Diana died on her trip. 

Her second daughter, Fiona, is the sole beneficiary of her entire estate. Diana’s executrix, Mary, is now the registered owner of all three properties. Alice still has the transfer forms in her possession. It turns out that the form to transfer The Cottage to Alice was improperly executed by Diana and it could not have been registered in any event. The form to transfer The Villa to Robert was valid and registrable until the moment that Diana died. 

What rights, if any, does Alice have to The Cottage? What rights, if any, does Robert have to The Villa? Would it make any difference if Alice had been appointed as Diana’s executrix, instead of Mary?
