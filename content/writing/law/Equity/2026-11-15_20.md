1. What is meant by a 'gratuitous transfer'? Name three modes by which such a transfer can be accomplished.

!ANSWER
A transfer done by volunteers is gratuitious. They have not put up anyting or any detrimental reliance to affect a transaction. The three methods are: 

Donor can make an absolute gift by transferringn the legal title to the donee. (Absolute gift)

Settlor transfers the legal title of the property to a third party trustee for the purpsoe of holding on trust for another. (Express trust method 1)

Settlor declares that he or she holds property on trust for another (Express trust method 2) self declare themselves to be a trustee for another. 

2. Why, in Milroy v Lord, did Lord Turner refuse to contemplate construing an imperfect gift as a completely constituted trust? 

!ANSWER
Because equity will not perfect an imperfect gift. 

Milroy v. Lord -> In order to create a settlement binding on the settlor they must do everything that must be done according to the nature of the property in order for the settlement to bind them. 
 
3. Diana owned three houses (in fee simple). She wanted to give one house (‘The Cottage’) to her daughter, Alice, and another house (‘The Villa’) to her son, Robert. Diana executed the forms to be used to transfer the two properties to Alice and Robert respectively. She then left on a round the world adventure holiday. 

On the day she left, Diana delivered both transfer forms to Alice and asked her to take care of the registration to complete the transfers to both properties. Alice agreed to perform these tasks; however, as a result of a personal crisis, she forgot to submit the forms for registration. Diana died on her trip. 

Her second daughter, Fiona, is the sole beneficiary of her entire estate. Diana’s executrix, Mary, is now the registered owner of all three properties. Alice still has the transfer forms in her possession. It turns out that the form to transfer The Cottage to Alice was improperly executed by Diana and it could not have been registered in any event. The form to transfer The Villa to Robert was valid and registrable until the moment that Diana died. 

!ANSWER
Mascall v. Mascall -> So long as the donor has done all of what is required of them then that is sufficient for a constructive bare trust, which would lead to a gift. However the donor had not done so, so therefore there are issues in this case. 

Relevance -> For Robert then that would be sufficient since all of the donor's formalities had been so done, and therefore the gift would be most likely upheld. 

Pennington v. Waine -> However in this case the question was doing everything to effect a transfer and it would be unconscionable to go back on the gift in that case because the donor was under the impression they had done everything to affect a transfer. 

Relevance -> May be unconscionable to fail to uphold the gift, but in Pennington there was a small amount of reliance with the donee becoming the director of a company. 

Zettal v. Kaye -> Case where the donor was transferring shares to the donee, filled out one of the stock transfer forms correctly but not the second. Held that equity could not uphold the gift of the second share.

Relevance -> Where formalities are not complied with equity will not intervene to perfect an imperfect gift.

Khan v. Mahmood -> Nephew wanted to make a gratuitous transfer of interest in a co-owned house to uncle. Non observance of formalities required for transfer. Unconscionable to allow resile from the gift. Focussed on Donee's conduct rather than Donee's reliance on donors intentions, intentions are revealed through his actions. 

Relevance -> Great deal of fact specifics and also issues with the ratio being controversial.

What rights, if any, does Alice have to The Cottage? What rights, if any, does Robert have to The Villa? Would it make any difference if Alice had been appointed as Diana’s executrix, instead of Mary? 

!ANSWER

Rule in Strong v. Bird -> Extinction of debt between the parties to extend to gifts as well as debts. 'IF it can be shown that the donor intended to make an immediate gift to an inter vivos donee, and that intention endures until death, if the donee is appointed to the role of executor then that combination will mean that the gift will be perfected. Takes effect on death of the donor.'
