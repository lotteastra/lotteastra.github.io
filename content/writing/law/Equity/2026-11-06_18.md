Knight v. Knight -> Establishes the three certainties.

INTENTION

Trusts:

Marcus v. Marcus -> Case where a trust was for children but one of the children was not biologically related to the settlor, without the settlor's knowledge, the context of a given word will be broadened based on contextual factors.

Capacity to create a trust is also of note here as without the capacity to create a trust then there can be no trust. AGE IS RELEVANT.

North v. Wilkinson -> A trust creates onerous duties on the trustee and therefore to bind them the words need to be clear. Trusts are also irrevocable once made.

Paul v. Constance -> Key test is if the settlor/testator intended to impose an obligation of this type and as such the specific wording is crucial.

Tito v. Waddell no. 2 -> The word trust is not necessary for the creation of a trust, merely the intention to create an obligation of this nature is sufficient. 

Pearson v. Lehman bros -> Test is that of obligation.

Precatory words are something that you need to be careful of.

Desire, wish or request or confident are unsufficient, there has to be clear meaning that there is a requirement to create this trust. Not a mere moral obligation. 

Re Adams and the Kensington Vestry -> I give, devise and bequeath all my real and personal estate and effects unto and to the absolute use of my dear wife in full confidence that she will do what is right as to the disposal thereof between my children either in her lifetime or by will after her decease. This is not good enough to create a trust, 'I give,' 'absolute use,' 'full confidence,' 'in her lifetime or by will,' is far to vague to impose an obligation. 

Contrast 

Comisky v. Bowring-Hanbury -> IN full confidence that she will make such use of it as I should have made myself and that at her death she will devise it to such one or more of my nieces as she may think fit and in default of any dispostition by her thereof by her will... I hereby direct that all my estate and property acquired by her under this my will shall at her death be equally dvided among the surviving said nieces. This is sufficient. 'in full confidence,' is present, but the obligations are clearer and more obslute, 'shall,' stronger than otherwise.

Re Steele's WIll Trust -> Also relevant if a lawyer drafted a will in line with a prior case where a trust was recognized, then the nature of that will be sufficient to construe a trust.

Gill v. Thind -> Person who alleges there is a trust bares the burden of proof to prove it. Where there is a document then it is a question of law if the words used are sufficient to impose this obligation. If oral then first there are questions of fact regarding what was said to whom. 

Re Gulbenkian -> It is the duty of the court by the exercise of its judicial knowledge and experience in the relevant matter. Innate common sense and desire to make sense of the settlor's expressed intentions however obscure and ambigious the langauge that may have been used to give a reasonable meaning to that language if it can do so without doing violence to it. 

Gold v. Hill -> Told solicitor to make arrangements if anything happened to look after his mistress and children. 

Jones v. Lock -> Father returned from a business trip without a gift for baby. I am going to put it away for him. That case. 'Loose conversations of this sort,' being sufficient to declare a trust seen as dangerous.

Paul v. Constance -> 'As much your money as mine,' sufficient to create a trust. 

Rowe v. Prance -> D had an extramaritial affair with C and D told C that D was going to divorce partner, sell home and use proceeds to buy a yacht. D did not divorce, or sell, but did buy a yacht. Successfully found that the yacht was held on trust for both of them through describing the boat as 'ours.' 

Agnew and Douglas -> Where there is a self trust, two intentions need to be established, composite intention to hold rights in trust property for the benefit or another to assume the obligations of a trustee, and that by their words or actions the trust should be constituted immediately. 

Henry v. Hammond -> Reluctance to find a trust relationship in a commercial context. See also Neste Oy v. Lloyd's Bank plc

North v. Wilkonson -> In a commercial context it is also necessary to consider if the parties meant a contract or the more onerous relationships through a trust.

Wang v. Darby -> A trust of cyrptocurrency was not recognized because it ran contrary to the business relationship of the parties, that being buying and selling of cryptocurrency.

R v. Clowes -> Investors induced to invest in a scheme by misrepresentation in brochures which stated that client money would be securely invested and kept in separate accounts and that the clients would be beneficial owners of these accounts. Therefore separation of assets in a commercial contexts generally points towards the finding of a trust.

Re Lewis's of Leicester ltd -> Company which traded as a department store licensed other traders to trade within a shop. The money made by a trader like this was paid into tills controlled by the company and then into a separate bank account and at the end of the month the profits would be paid to the traders minus commission. This separation of assets demonstrated sufficient intention to create a trust.

Imperfect gifts cannot be saved by turning them into a trust as the nature of an obligation is different (See Jones v. Lock)

Marley v. Rawlings at para 19 -> A will should be interpreted like a contract.

S.21 Administration of Justice Act 1982 allows the court to use extrinsic evidence where relevant.

Re Freud -> Court had to construe a will to determine whether residuary estate was held on trust for any chilldren. One clause stated that the residuary estate was to be given to the solicitor and one of the children. S.21 was invoked to clear this up where an earlier version of the will stated it was to be held on trust and since the wording had changed then that defeated the finding that the words written in the will as it stood meant to construe a trust.

Loring v. Woodland Trust -> Testatrix made a will in 2001 and left to children and grandchildren such a sum that was exempt from inheritance tax. Which at the time was 325k, however the rules changed. It was held that the rules changed but the language hadn't annd therefore should be construed under the new rules.

Re Hand's Will Trust -> Children included adopted children.
