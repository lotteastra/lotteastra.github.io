Three certainties. The beneficiary has a right over the property. The nature of the interest is proprietary in nature. Beneficiary principle. 

Settlor transfers 100k to a trustee in order for T to investigate if ghosts or aliens exist. The trustee has legal title but who has been given the beneficial interest over the money.  Who would enforce and how if these terms were not complied with and how do we know that this purpose has been met, and what if this money has been met. Also these assets must not exist in this way in perpetuity. 

Beneficiary principle. For a trust to be valid it must have a human beneficiary who can enforce the trust for people not purposes. There are however exceptions. 

Perpetuity rule as well. At some point the money will be vested in someone. 

Morice v. Bishop -> Testator tried to create a trust for "objects of benevolence and liberality as the trustee in his own discretion shall approve of."

Found that trust could not be constituted for no person. 

Re Enddacott -> A trust for some 'useful memorial to myself,' there was no ascertainable beneficiary and therefore not enough. 

Re Shaw -> GBS wanted to leave funds to investigate possibility of 40 letter alphabet, but there was no human beneficiary and therefore there was no trust. 

Saunders v. Vautier -> If a group of beneficiaries all with a vested interest in a trst and an absolute vested interest, together, or as one if only one beneficiary can act to collapse the trust. Highlights the interests and nature of the beneficiary. Not about giving effect to settlors intention but the beneficiary. It is a proprietary interest as well. 

Justifications -> A trust that cannot be enforced must fail and only beneficiaries can enforce so without them then a trust cannot operate. 

Re Astor's Settlement Trust -> "Good understanding between nations and preservation of indepencene and integrity of newspapers" purpose trust is void because nobody can enforce it. 

The concern is enforcement. 

David Hayton -> The underlying principle is that for there to be a trust obligation there needs to be some person intended by the settlor to have locus standi to enforce the trustee's duties.

Trustee is a powerful position and if there is no beneficiary then they can act as they please. 

If a trustee has duties then someone must have a right. 

In some jurisdictions however this can be relaxed and there is an independent enforcer who could exist. Not in English law. 

Re Astor -> If the purpsoes are charitable, in equitable rights then the rights are based inter personam and therefore need a specific person to be able to act. 

Their interest over the assets and allow the settlors interest to be honoured. Though beneficiaries can ultimately collapse the trust. 

Minimizes damage to the economy. 

Perpetuity, certainty and capriciousness are all problems with purpose trusts.

Re Denley -> If the trust is expressed in the form of a purpose it may still be valid if it is for the direct or indirect benefit of one or more ascertainable individuals. 'The said land shall be maintained and used as and for the purpose of a recreation or sports ground primarily for the benefit of the employees of the company and secondarily for the benefit of such other person or persons if any as the trustees may allow to use the same.'

Goff J. in Re Denley 

"I think that there may be a purpose or object trust the carrying out of which would benefit an individual or individuals, where the benefit is so indirect or intangible or which is otherwise framed as not to give those persons any locus standi to apply to the court to enforce the trust in which case the beneficiary principle would as it seems to be apply to invalidate the trust quite apart from any question of uncertainty or perpetuity. The present is not in my judgement of that character and it will be seen that the trust deed expressly states that subject to any rules and regulations made by the trustees the employees of the company shall be entitled to the use and enjoyment of the land. IN MY JUDGEMENT THE BENEFICIARY PRINCIPLE OF RE ASTOR IS CONFINED TO PURPOSE OR OBJECT TRUSTS WHICH ARE ABSTRACT OR IMPERSONAL. The objection is not that the trust is for a purpose or object per se but that there is no beneficiary WHERE THEN THE TRUST THOUGH EXPOSED AS A PURPOSE IS DIRECTLY OR INDIRECTLY FOR THE BENEFIT OF AN INDIVIDUAL OR INDIVIDUALS IT SEEMS TO ME THAT IT IS IN GENERAL OUTSIDE THE MISCHIEF OF THE BENEFICIARY PRINCIPLE." 

In effect where a beneficiary is ascertainable even by way of a purpose then a trust may be constituted.

Unincorporated associations

What is an unincorporated association?

Definitions from Conservative and Unionist Central office v. Burrell 

'Two or more persons bound together for one or more common purpose not being business purposes by mutual undertakings each having mutual duties and obligations in an organization which has rules which identify in whom control of its funds rests and on what terms and which can be joined or left at will.' Lawton LJ in Burrell

Not just an organization, but one with rules. 

Two or more persons

A non-business common purpose

The undertaking of mutual duties and mutual obligations between members

A body of governing rules

A fluctuating body of members

No corporate personality like a company. When property belongs to this association tit must be held by the members on behalf of the association.

What is the nature of how these things are held. 

Leahy v. AG for New SOuth Wales

Property a house with 20 rooms should be held on trust for such order of nuns as the Catholic Church or the Christian Brothers as my executors and trustees shall select.' 

Order of nuns is a UA, gift not charitable but saved by specific legislation in the jurisdiciton. 

No valid trust because no intention to give individuals and violates beneficiary principle and perpetuity rule. 

If no trust then it must be a gift to the association

In law a gift to a UA is a gift to its member. 

They are either given to the donee to be held on trust for specific members of the association or an absolute gift to use as they wish with having no regard to the intention. 

This is a problem because the gift is motivated by a specific purpose. 

Neville Estates v. Madden -> down

Three possible constructions of gifts to a UA

A gift to the members of the association as co-owners so that any member could sever their share and claim it whether or not they continue to be a member. (absolute gift)

A gift to the members of the UA subject to their contractual rights and liabilities towards one another as members of a UA, and a member cannot sever their share (contract holding theory)

Gift to members of UA but to be held in trust to be applied for the purposes of the UA as a quasi corporate entity. Will fail unless UA is a charity. 

Re Recher's Will Trusts -> Testatrix wanted to leave part of estate to the London and Proncicial Anti-Vivisection society. 

"In the absense of words which purport to impose a trust, the legacy is a gift to the members beneficially not as joint tenants or as tenants in common so as to entitle each member to an immediate distributive share but as an accretion to the funds which are the subject matter of the contract which the members have made inter se." Brightman J at 539

This did fail but because the association had failed
