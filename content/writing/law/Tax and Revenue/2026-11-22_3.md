title: Tax Employment/PAYE
---

Employment tax is in general governed by ITEPA 2003, replacing parts of the TA 1988 and other statutes that dealt with Schedule E.  

ITEPA defines employment in S.4 'includes, in particular (a)any employment under a contract of service (b) any employment under a contract of apprenticeshipi and (c) any employment in the service of the Crown.' 

This is a rough section, being both a non-exhaustive list, and also 'employment under a contract,' is not a legal contract. There exists no such thing.

Earnings is defined in S.62(2) to 'include not only gratuities and incidental benefits but also 'emolments.' This means benefits in kind. S.62 however, only taxable if it is convertible into money. Found in the benefits code. 

S.336 details edeductable expenses in calculating taxable earnings if they are incurred wholly necessary and exclusively in the performance of the duties of the office or employment.

Owen v. Pook -> (1)  (Lord Wilberforce dubitante, Lord Pearson dissenting) that, on the basis that the travelling allowance paid by the hospital committee was a reimbursement for actual expenditure, it was not an emolument of the taxpayer's office or employment and did not fall to be charged." "A perk is merely a casual emolument additional to regulate salary or wages." Equally travelling from patient to patient was deductable but not travelling from home to hospital as it was the taxpayer's choice to live in such a place. 

Employment income counts from employments but also offices. Employment and an office are equally not mutually exclusive either. An office is more than just a job description, it is independent of the identity of the present holder. 

S.62 -> Earnings arer money or money's worth. 

Tenant v. Smith -> Accomodation for a bank manager required he live above the bank couldn't assign the lease, only his immediate family. Since the benefit was not convertable, it was not money's worth and therefore not taxable. 

Heaton v. Bell -> Car manufacturer, if you worked and set aside savings you got a discounted car from the factory. Held to be a convertable benefit and therefore subject to taxation. The fact that salary was set aside meant that this was taxable. 

Weight v. Salmon -> Employee given compensated shares as part of employment and these shares were ultimately part of earnings. Therefore subject to taxation. 

Abbot v. Philibin -> Employees were given an option to buy shares at a set price and years later could by at that earlier price. Then sell on the market. House of Lords said that the option was a benefit but since they bought the option at the time was fair market value. Benefit held not to be taxable with there being no source. Option was the benefit not the shares. 

Parliament allowed shares option with preferred taxation so long as it lasts a certain term. 

UBS v. HMRC -> Wanted to give bankers bonuses. Allows employees to cash them earlier, within 18 months, but SUpreme Court said that it was taxable for all of it. The exemption for shares following from Weight v. Salmon was to be construed where the transaction was for a commercial purpose.  

Wilkins v. Rogerson -> Voucher to get a suit from a fancy suit placce. Court held that it was a benefit since it was ordinary clothes and not a  uniform however the value was difficult to determine and was held to be the second hand value of the suit on the market. 

Beak v. Robson -> Scheme to get money to a director. Office holder and therefore employed. Part of the contract was to not work for a competitor. The payment to not do so was not taxable at the time but would be now under revised S.225 ITEPA. 

Vaughn v. Neil IRC -. Barrister paid to give up bar, held to be professional and not employed. 

Nicoll v. Austin -> Large house and maids are part of business and held that the employee could be taxed on this as it was not part of his duties. This was the case where a guy bought a huge house that was unrelated to his duties and filled it with maids and then tried to get a deduction on it. 

Richardson v. Warrall -> Company card used to buy petrol sometmies for work or personal, and it was held to be a taxable benefit in that case. 

Rendell v. Went -> Employee charged with death by dangerous driving company paid for best representation and the courts held that this was a taxable benefit. 

McKnight v. Sheppard -> However rperesentation for discipline hearing at work was not a taxable benefit as it was in the course of employment and not money's worth. 

S.63 ITEPA includes the benefit code. S.217 ITEPA means in effect it applies to all. 

There are two questions. 

1. Is there a convertable benefit

2. Does the employer play more, if so taxable for the difference. 

If the employer gives a convertable benefit then it will be taxable. There are exemptions, in particular de minmis under 50 pounds. Bus services and canteen services when open to all employees. Annual parties as well but once per year and 150 pounds per head. Vouchers for childcare and incidental overnight expenses are also allowable. 

Pepper v. Hart -> Involved a private boarding school where if you were a teacher there you could send your children there at a reduced rate. Should the benefit be taxed at the marginal rate of provision of schooling or at the rate that would have been for the general public. HoL majority said that it should be the amount that the general public would have paid rather than the marginal rate. Though there was a dissenter that it should have been the marginal rate 

Cars have special rules on engine size where below 160mm they will not be taxed as a benefit. This applies to personal and non-carpoole dcars. 

Loans free or no interest loans tax will be charged as a benefit on the difference between the official and preferential rate.

Wicks v. Firth ICI predocessor system for children of employees. If they went to university and did well at A-Levels. The revenue said that this was a benenfit. Cash benefit. But scholarships would be okay. S.211-215 give the rules that are general on this element. 

Expenses are prima fascie taxable but there are ways of mitigating this. In particular S.336, expenses that are wholly exclusively and necessarily in the performance of duties will be allowable. Pandemic working at home expenses included this. 

Smith v. Abbot -> Journalists given an allowance to buy competitor newspapers but it was held taht this expenditure did not help in the performance of duties since those duties are to write the news and not read it.

Brown v. Bullrack -> Bank manager who said it was compulsory to be the member of a gentlemen's club since this was Mayfair and that is where he took his clients, and this was not accepted, held to be taxable. Not wholly and exclusively.

Humbles v. Brooks -> History teacher went to night school and tried to claim this as his expenses but held not so as he was hired to teach and not to learn. 

HMRC v. Bannerjee -> Medical registrar had to do traning contract to become a consultant, wne on a course and held that since this was a contract for training then this would be an allowable expense. However dissent in this case that this was to improve professionally and not help with the performance of duties. 

Case where -> Town clerk tried to claim expenses on going to have a meal at a restaurant before taking the minutes of the meeting they were assigned to. Not allowable. 

S.337 concerns travel expenses. 

Ricketts v. Colfton -> Recorder of Portsmouth lived in London but went to that city in the fullfillment of his duties. Held that the commute was outside of the duties since there was nothing necessary about living in London and his duty was to dispense justice to the citizens of Portsmouth. 

Pook v. Owen -> The GP case

Taylor v. Provan -> Fixer paid expenses were taxable. Lived in Canada but came to the UK to do his job and those expenses were not allowable. 

S.338 exempts necessary travel 

Kirkwood v. Vevais -> Mostly worked from home but came in once per week. 

Simpson v. Tale -> Professional body subscriptions are generally not allowable expenses but S.343-S.346 ITEPA give a closed allowable list. 

Payments at the end of employment. For many of these payments there is no source. Personal injury, garden leave, payment in lieu of notice, payment for wrongful dismissal etc. 

There is a code in S.401 to 406 payments in lieu of notice are taxable though there is no source. S.306 exceptions for death or injury. 

Murphy v. HMRC -> Accountant told to leave and then sued former employer for psychological damage. Held that while there were allowances for personal injury there were not for hurt feelings or discrimination and therefore tax was allowed on those payments. 

Are they earnings? Are they paid by a third party are they convertable? Refer to specific statutory provisions.
