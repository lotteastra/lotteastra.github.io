---
title: Case I UK Uncut v HMRC and Goldman Sachs [2013] EWHC  1283(Admin)
---

Facts: Application for judicial review by a pressure group related to the conduct of HMRC in relation to a settlement reached between HMRC and Goldman Sachs. In the settlement GS had refused to pay National Insurance contributions like a group of other companies, but those companies settled as a group and were absolved of having to pay interest on what they whould have had to pay. GS held out until there was a meeting regarding the settlement, where the Private Secretary of HMRC erroniously believed the legal position and formalities were different than they were and accepted a settlement without the interest paymenst. This is in spite of the fact that GS had waited another 5 years. The settlement was eventually approved but with a lot of back and forth and concerns about the relationship between GS and HMRC. 

Reasons for Dispute: The pressure group brought judicial review on the basis of irrelevant considerations, pointing towards avoiding embarassment akin to Padfield, as well as preserving a working relationship between GS and HMRC. These were dismissed on the grounds that the working relationship could be a relevant consideration and it was for the decision maker to decide that. Similarly the claims of embarassment were dismisseed as there were chiefly other reasons for the decision. There was also a claim made on the basis that it violated an internal policy within HMRC however it was held that because the GS issue was 'bespoke,' then the policy, specifically the litigation strategy was not appropriate for that. There was an argument on the basis of equality, that all taxpayers of a certain class should be treated the same, and the different treatment between the companies that settled and GS was held up as an example. However following R (E) v. Nottinghamshire NHS trust, equality just means there must be rational reasons to explain why differing groups are treated differently. Discussion also of Fleet Street Casuals case and the principle of equality at large
