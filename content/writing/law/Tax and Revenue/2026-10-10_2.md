Singer family case -> There was a dispute about the tax of an estate that was in New York, they were told to and did pay tax one year but the next year they found new evidence and appealed and the next time they were not taxable, so each year is divisible. 

Income tax comes in various different sources, there is,

Employment (Schedule E)

Pension (Schedule E)

Trading and Professional Icome (Schedule D cases I-II)

Property (Scehdule A)

Savings and investment (Gilts Schedule C, Schedule D case III for bank interest and Pure profit, Schedule F for company dividens)

Misc (Schedule D case VI)

Higgs v. Olivier -> Actor in WW2 starred in Henry V film on the understanding that if he acted in no more films for the next 5 years he would be paid money as a result. Held that the payment was not taxable as he was not working as an actor when he was paid it. 

Bray v. Best -> Trust established for employees to be paid out on the basis of years of service in employement. Issue was taxation, they should be taxed when they actually recieve it and not when the money is accrueing or expected. 

Mitchell and Eden v. Ross -> NHS doctors who ran private practices and tried to offset expenses against their taxable employment income, found that they could not do that. 

HMRC v. HFFX llp -> Partners in a company were paid out money after two years, tried to claim it was employment income and Bray v. Best but the UKSC said it was taxable as misc income.

Remember personal allowance, and how it tapers off after 100k

PAYE allows taxes to be paid in real time to HMRC, and HMRC sends a code to a company for the taxation that can be expected and then the company deducts from the paycheque. 

ITEPA is the act of importance.

CLinch v. Edwards temporary appointment as a planning officer not an office holder but a contract of employment. If he were an office holder then that would be professional income and not employment income. 

Market Investigators v. Minister of Social Security -> Referees tried to argue that they were not employees since they can say no to showing up, and the company has no control over their rulings, but looked at hollistically it was still employment. 

Autoclave v. Belcher -> Office clerks for an office and tried to argue profesionals but were found to be employees, look to the substance of the contract. 

Fall v. Hichin -> ballet dancer who tried to claim that they were a professional because despite their 30 week contract they could still seek other employment during that time. The court said no because it was such a substantial part of the year that he was an employee. 

Hall v. Lormier -> TV producer with no overall control and equipment provided and no finiancial risk, but the CA said that he was a professional because of how he was engaged. 

PGMOL v. HMRC -> Referees case

HMRC v. Bluecrest Partners -> Partners must be engaged in management in order to be taxed as professionals rather than taxed as employees. Look to the substance. 

ICI employment relocation -> Not employment income as though employment was the proximate cause, the real cause of payment was the relocation itself and not the employment overall. 

Hamblett v. Godfrey -> GCHQ case, where workers were paid to give up their trade union memberships and benefits, found to be employment income as trade union rights are so linked to employment. 

Mairs v. Haughey -> A shipyard in NI as at risk of a US buyout, there was a fund set up if it was to become insolvent and held that this wasn't taxable as employment income. Not so linked to employment.
