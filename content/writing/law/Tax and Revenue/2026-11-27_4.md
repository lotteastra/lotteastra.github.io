S.205 Insurance premiums not allowed as expense against a capital gain. TCGA. Household insurance you have against a building however those are specifically not allowable.

Losses calculated the same way as gains S.16 TCGA, same system as corporation tax. Can't exaggerate losses or gains. Subject to TAAR targetted anti-avoidance rule, under S.16A.

Appropriations to and from trading stock. These are booked at market value unless the transaction would be exempt from CGT. Fiscal considerations are put to one side in considering whether item bought as trading stock.

New Angel Court v. Adam -> New Angel Court contained some old inland revenue offices which where appropriated. 

Life assurance contracts are generally taxed under income tax, and are exempt unless the contract has been bought for actual consideration. S.210 TCGA amended by the Finance Act 2003. If the life assurance is traded then they become capital gains and are dealt with that.

S.103KA -> Carried interest, hedge funds. Where you have interest on investments and instead of keeping it you reinvest it. In the US this is taxed as Capital Gains and not income. Argument was that it should be taxed as income and not capital gain. We tax it and that makes the UK less attractive.

Reliefs from CGT

Holdover relief, found in S.165(1) TCGA, where a business asset is gifted, the holdover relief is that the donee is taken to acquire the asset at the price value that it was acquired by the donor. Justified through family businesses. Those tend to last 2-3 generations and then they get sold.

Holdover Relief also applies for IHT as a result of S.260 TGCA. Transfer to trustees would be a disposal but would also be subject to IHT relief. The CGT will not apply in those circumstances.

Rollover relief, where a trade is carried on then one can trade old assets for new assets. When there is relief it is given on a no gain no loss basis. Say you have a large office, and you switch for a small office. You would be subject to capital gains. There is a list of assets to which rollover relief applies. S.152 and 155. Usual list of business assets. 

Entrepreneurs' Relief S.169H from 2010 to 2020, gave a lifetime relief to businessmen who built their business, and then sold, but a 10 million pound limit. Only 10% CGT on the first 10 million of gains. Doesn't apply to corporations and a minimum investment of 5% which excludes shares as an investment. When it started the lifetime maximum was 500k but it was raised a lot. Gradually put up and up. Until 2020. Now there is a 2020 renamed business asset disposal relief. Schedule 3 paragraph 7 finance act 2020. Lifetime limit reduced to 1 million. 

Founder's Relief from 2027, for business assets held for 5 years in which the resident taxpayer was concerned, relief for the first 10 million gain in that CGT reduced to 10%. 

Only or main residence relief. Found in S.222 TCGA, in a sense a rollover relief. If you have a main residence where you live and you sell it, it is not subject to capital gains tax. The rationale is that you have to live somewhere so you will go and buy another house once you sell. Generally you might get a more expensive house when you sell too. There are issues, if there was residence etc.

A matter of fact for residence, but a temporary stay is insufficient. Only the residence part of a building qualifies so if there is a shop or office as part of the building this does not qualify for relief. 

Makins v. Elson -> Dwellinghouse extends to caravanas and possibly houseboats. 

224(3) does not cover if a property is owned for the purpose of making profit from the sale or the gain is attributed to improvements on dwelling house. Revenue not so interested in applying this section.

Main residence -> Where a person has two residences only one can be his main residence. Under S.222(5) a person can elect which one is the main residence for the purpose of the releif. After such election a further electoin can be made by giving notice to HMRC but not as to effect the position earleir than 2 years prior to this section. 

MPs used to buy dwellinghouse paid for by Parliamentary allowances. This amount, and then they chose that residence as their main residence. When they lost they sold this residence and made a profit for which they did not account for Parliament. 

S.222(6) Only one relief for a husband and wife living together. Exemptions if away from residence for work related reasons. Also applies if dwelling occupied under the terms of settlement. 

Morgan v. HMRC -> Moved in for two months to get property ready to rent residence. Was the residence used as the centre of operations for a person. Found that it was the residence.

S Bailey v. HMRC -> Wanted to reside but had to sell it, but he had moved in. Held to be sufficient. 

Moore v. HMRC -> Man having an affair, kicked out of his house, bought a house subsequently living in it for a few months. Clearly temporary. Denied relief in those circumstances because after he moved out sold property and made a gain. 

Extent of the dwelling house, includes a garden up to 0.5 hectare. But where a larger area is required that is also permitted. 

Lewis v. Lady Rook -> Case where there was a cottage. Such a large house with a large garden and you had to provide gardener accomodation. CA applied a test, whether this was within the curtilage of and appurtenant to the main house. Therefore no relief. 

Markey v. Saunders -> Staff bungalow was taxable. 

Varty v. Lynes -> Someone had a large garden, sold the house and a small part of the garden but retained a large part of the garden. Later sold that to a developer, held that there was no exemption for the second one because the timing had gone. Therefore disentitled to main residence relief. 

Longson v. Baker -> Paddock for horses not necessary for enjoyment of the property but a garden used within the hosue is included even if physically separate. 

Honour v. Norris -> Case involving flats. Mr Norris bought 2-3 flats adjacent to each other. Used it as though it were one residence. Ate in one, lived in another, slept in but another, said it was one residence but the court found to the contrary. Relief on one of the flats but not the other two. 

Wakeling v. Pearce -> Garden separated by public path included. 

HMRC v. Lee -> Slightly odd case. Someone bought a house, but only moved in after about a year. Because there was building, lived for 4-5 years and then decided to sell it. Was the first year part of the period of residence or did they have to attribute that first year to CGT. Held that the words of the statute mention ownership and that is the key thing. Since it was owned and then used as a residence then that was sufficient. 

If there is a property that is occupied, and then rented out. If you have got a property, you leave and then let it out, and you pay tax on that, then you can have a further relief. 

Suppose flat bought for 50k in 1985, movse to a new house in 1995 when the value of the flat is 100k, and then he sells the flat at 250k. Gain is 200k the relief is on 10 years plus 6 months over the total ownership, so the relief is 110k, and there is a chargeable gain of 90k. If during the unoccupied period the house was let out for 5 years at a commercial rent. The gain attributable to that period is 90k then only 10k of that is chargeable gain. You can spend ages on this. 

What needs to be said is that it is a very fertile area for examiners. People with two houses, or houses abroad. Extent of the houses. 

S.256(1) exempts capital gains made by a charity. S.256(2) provides for a situation where a property ceases to be held on charitable trust. 

Art/Trusts S.30 provides some protection for important works of art. Considered more with regard to IHT. Trusts are also later.
