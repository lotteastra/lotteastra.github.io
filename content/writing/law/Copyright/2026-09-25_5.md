1. What are the most frequently advanced justifications for the existence, and scope, of copyright protection? Can you see any flaws with these justifications?

Spence highlights two justifications. Firstly economic justifications associated primarily with the US as the constitution does explicitly mention 'for the promotion of arts,' as the reasoning behind the existence of copyright law. As such the argument is it should exist in order to promote innovation even if there is a cost to the economy through the creation of a monopoly correcting the market failure of someone being able to benefit by copying without having to pay for the innovation itself is worth that. Spence does highlight a flaw in this argument particularly related to works that are of considerable value by themselves and are in no need of help by a monopoly being imposed on their reproduction particularly works of fine visual arts. Though as the Upholstery case demonstrates what is fine art is a fine distinction. 

The second justification is moral or 'deontological,' as Spence puts it. Arguing that authors have a right to their work. This has been justified both through Locke's labour mixing theory, or through the argument that works exist as part of the author's personality. It is argued that unauthorized reproductions do not harm the original author, though Spence counters by mentioning that a neo-nazi group using artwork could shift the meaning of that work in a way the author does not desire. 

The UDR also protects this second justification. It is also associated with France and more continental systems. 

See also the private property/neoliberal justification highlighted in the textbook.

I would dispute the moral justifications in the sense that while Spence does derride it as postmodernism, the fact is that authors are always drawing on a broader culture. While authors argue they do create something themselves they do not do so ex nihilo. It overly priveleges the position of the author, and it has no concerns for the wider social order which an author inhabits, in particular the wellbeing of that society, which is impoverished by granting monopolies over ideas in perepetuity. This creation of a monopoly system stifles innovation and creativity more broadly. 

I would agree moreso with the economic rationale as it demonstrates concern for wider social order beyond the author, a social order which the author themselves relied on in order to make their work. Everything from the words of the language they use, to the genre conventions they rely on. As such the economic rationale by this concern makes more sense. It also lacks the moralism of the other justification.


2. Applying the reasoning that you have encountered in your reading on justifications, consider the following claims for copyright protection:

(i)	X plc creates expensive perfumes. It has come up with a new product, “Freesia” and believes that it should be entitled to protect its scent.

It should be entitled provided they incurred costs in deriving their new scent, and that it is genuinely original. Further this protection should be limited. The scent needs to be sufficiently different from any natural scent or any scent that is easy to make so as to avoid the risk of innocent infringement. In a sense the copyright should only be granted insofar as it allows X plc to continue its business through collecting rent on the idea that they are responsible for. Also I didn't know Musk was into perfume but I guess it is in the name. 

(ii)	Concerned about the rising incidence of deepfakes, Denmark is considering the introduction of a form of copyright protection for individuals’ identity. 

There is considerable danger in this proposal in the sense that it would limit the ability of the public to comment on public figures or take photos or selfies with them. A harmless selfie could become copyright infringement should this come to pass. It would also prevent any filming in public as if you give away the copyright of your likeness for one clip then they could perhaps claim ownership more broadly. This is a ruinious proposal and it would make operating in public nearly impossible without having to pay even more rent to celebrities. 

(iii)	Yolande is an author. She is unhappy that she receives no income from the second-hand copies of her novels and thinks that the law should be amended to allow her to share in the profits of the online marketplaces from which on which her books are sold.

Boo hoo she should write more and better books. If she is unsatisfied with her returns then the answer is to innovate further and not try to squeeze more from the consumer. Indeed an author would also benefit by their works being more openly avaialble and new fans may emerge which she could sell her next works to.
