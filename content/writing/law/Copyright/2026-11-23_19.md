Moral rights are a kind of right reserved only for the copyright owner. Economic rights are the other stuff. 

We don't care that much about moral rights in the UK. There are only 3 rights. 

1. Right of attribution. The owner of moral right has a right to be identified as the author. 

2. Right of false attribution if you don't want to be attributed then you can move away from it. 

3. Right of integrity. Any harm that could arise to your representation through a work then you have a right to stop that. 

Very subjective for the third element. What are the key justifications for moral rights. Because it is your intellectual creation. Preservation of cultural heritage too. You can also pursue claims in tort as well.

Moral rights cannot be transferred though economic rights can. 

Right of attribution S.77-79 CDPA can be waived in future. 

Applies to authorial works, but not to all entrepenurial works depending on the statute. 

Angel of the North -> The author of the work. Right of attribution, but you are responsible to assert your name in your work. Unless you want to produce it anonymously. If you do not do so then you cannot assert moral rights. 

Attribution name attached to the work.

Your name must be attached subsequently once it was attached to the first copy. This applies to translations and all versions of a work. In a song where a lot of people are involved it will be the whole list of people involved so like the Beyonce slide. Cannot be assigned. 

Ask for damages where moral rights are infringed. 

Right not to be named. Wrongly named as author to LDMA works. Right of attribution only rights 20 years after though. So less of an issue. Moral rights exist without any damages too. 

Right of integrity. You have protection from distortion of your work and it can be harmful of you. Harms your reputation. Owner reputation. Public perceptions will be taken into account. 

Form of treatment of your work. Internal structure of your work. Any kind of treatment, changing title, changing structure would be sufficient. Compare to the work as a whole. 

Snow v. Eaton -> Red ribbon tied around a sculpture was sufficient to find damage to integrity and therefore infringement of moral rights. 

Delves Broughton v. House of Harlot -> Cropping a photo could also damage the integrity in this way.

Confetti Records v. Warner Music UK -> Failed to prove that the act had damaged the claimant's reputation and that was fatal to finding a claim in that case. 

Boomerang investments v. Padgett -> Moral rights might be infringed, the right of integrity changing a word in a song. Even one word is sufficient. Owner and reputation is key. 

Work has been dealt with in circumstances under rwhich the author is protected from derogatory treatment. 

Honour a quality that combines respect being proud and honesty, repudation, the opinion that people have about what someone or something is like. 

In the course of employment however you are precluded from enforcing moral rights. S.82 CDPA deals with this exception here.
