Nature of the rights (1)


X is a talented and world-famous (but rather grumpy) novelist. She seeks your advice on the following scenarios (You can ignore the potential application of any of the “permitted acts” (exceptions and limitations) under the CDPA 1988).

Scenario A

X’s most famous literary work is a long and complex historical novel called Broad How. The work has many avid fans, some of whom have created artistic images based on the characters and events described in the work and have posted them on Broadly How & Why, a social media platform established by fans of the novel. X disapproves of this practice and wonders whether her rights in Broad How enable her to prohibit it. 

Translation is generally infringement

Scenario B

X has authorised the sale of the e-book version of Broad How via the e-book platform, Burn, from which readers download copies of purchased e-books. Burn’s standard terms of business state that customers acquire a licence to reproduce and store a copy of the novel on a limited number of personal devices for a limited period (seventy years). Nevertheless, several readers of Broad How have entered into agreements purporting to sell their copies of the e-book to Re-read Ltd, a company specialising in the sale of such “second-hand” e-books. X thinks that the activities of these customers and of Re-read are unlawful and wants them to be stopped. 

Scenario C

X has authorised the sale and distribution of Broad How in Finland, in a paperback edition with black and white line illustrations. Lotta, a celebrated Finnish craftsperson, has bought fifty copies of the book in this edition. She has decorated them by colouring in the line illustrations beautifully and giving them each an individual, hand-crafted and painted dust-cover. Lotta has exported these copies to the UK, where they are being sold as collectors’ pieces by the Primrose Hill Craft Centre Ltd (PHCC).
