Question One

At this stage of your studies in the subject, you may find some of the provisions difficult to grasp completely but you should be able to understand the position in outline. Consider the following questions:

(a)	How long does copyright last?

For artistic works 70 years after the death of the author, for entreupeniral works it varies however, films it is death of various people involved in the production of the work plus 70 years, for music it is 70 years from recording, broadcasts are 50 years from the broadcast, and published editions it is 25 years from the date of publication. 

(b)	How long should it last? 

When copyright terms in the United States had to be extended beyond 28 years only 15% of copyright holders decided to do so [Duke Centre for Public Domain] this demonstrates that even for those who do hold copyright the expected windfall isn't worth the hassle of extending the term. Hence it shows that in practice the extended terms do not increase innovation or the creation of new works. Rather as the building blocks for prior works become more fenced in, it becomes more and more impossible for original ideas to be expressed. The granting of a monopoly in the form of copyright is equally a very strong right and does not lead to optimal production as the act of creation and production are two different things, therefore a copyright holder could abuse their market power to their own benefit, extracting rent. Fyre in his paper talks about this too. 

Ultimately people need to express themselves. They do so in the form of ideas, and people do operate in co-operation with each other. The extent of monopoly rights granted by copyright undermines the ability for other people to rephrase ideas and make interesting new tales based off of them. Similarly the expiration of copyright does not lead to a work dying. People still publish new editions of shakespeare enjoying copyright protection to that end, while at the same time the length of copyright leads to that distribution problem. Works can go orphaned without distribution on account of the monopoly whereas in the past they could have proliferated more widely. 

Copyright should be limited to a single decade term. To not do so stifles ideas, leads to suboptimal distribution of those same ideas, and discourages innovation by allowing creators to simply make one work and then coast off of the residuals from that for a long duration. And that is presupposing success, the more likely outcome is that they make their work, make a sum, and then no more, and the work subsists orphaned with no new editions. Authors would stand to benefit if their works could reach wider proliferation as a result of shorter copyright terms. 

Question Two

In Shazam Productions Ltd v Only Fools The Dining Experience Ltd [2022] EWHC 1379 (IPEC), John Kimbell QC, sitting as a Deputy High Court Judge, held that the character of Del Trotter in the TV show Only Fools and Horses was protected by copyright. Read paragraphs [76]-[122] of his Judgment and answer the following questions:

(a)	Why was it important to the claimants in the case to establish that Del’s character was protected by copyright in its own right (in addition to the TV show as a whole)?

Because the supposed and ultimately found to be infringer was copying the character for the purpose of a performance. As such if the character could not be protected then there would be no infringement found. 

(b)	Why do you think that it might be problematic to grant copyright in “characters”?

A character is too vague an element. While it could be invisaged that there was a character who was specifically being copied as is the case in the Del Boy case, what of a similarly exuberant character with a penchant for cigars. There is a real danger that similar characters could be constricted away.

(c)	Why were the legal structures introduced under the European Union (Withdrawal) Act 2018 not considered in Shazam?

Marleasing principle. Where British law had to be read in a way that aligned with EU law, even if that interpretation was rather strained. This was abolished in the REULR in 2023. As such the EU's open list system had to be interpreted within the UK's closed list system.

(d)	If a case on equivalent facts were to arise today, how would it be decided?

It would not be decided the same way. The character of Del Boy does not fit into any of the closed list categories. It is not literary because while the character subsists in scripts they are not what the character is. The character is the performance of the character, in addition to the particular costume. As such, while an actor doing a performance that copies a given character could be infringement as that performance would be protected, the character in an abstract sense would not be protected. Similarly with the clothing. As such elements of the character would be protected but unlike in how the case was decided, the character as a whole would not be.
