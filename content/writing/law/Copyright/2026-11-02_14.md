REPRODUCTIVE RIGHTS

Copying

S.17 confers the oldest rights. Infringement takes place regardless of the duration or method. So long as a copy is made.

Technische Universität Darmstadt v. Eugen Ulmer KG -> Demonstrated that storing information on a USB was sufficientn to constitute an infringement of the right to copy.

Getty v. Stability AI -> Also applies with regard to putting information onto a cloud service.

R v. Higgs -> Equally accessing material online where a copy is only stored in the RAM is equally sufficient.

There is an argument that copyright should only protect work as a communication, and therefore the copy would need to be distributed in order to be infringement.

!LDMA works

Statutory Definition under S.17(2) and includes other methods of production. So scanning in a book would not be the same way but it would still constitute copying. 

Francis Day & Hunter v. Bron -> The works must be objectively similar. 

Davis (J & S) Holdings v. Wright Health Group -> Using the instructions to make something would not be copyright infringement even if it used a copyrighted literary work as the recipe. 

Sandman v. Panasonic -> Circuit diagram used to make the electronics described however may be infringement. 

Autospin (Oil Seals) v. Beehive Spinning -> Drawing a scene described in a novel would not be infringement on account of the fidelity however.

!Sound Recordings and Films

Reproduction for films and sound recordings is narrower than for LDMA works. 

R. Arnold 'Content Copyrights and Signal COpyrights: The case for a Rational Scheme of Protection,' (2011) 1 QMJIP 272 -> The protection protects the signal and not the content.

Creation Records v. News Group Newspapers -> Not the case with photographs where the copyright extends to the content and not only the specific recording.

As such copyright in a sound recording is not infringed by someone transcribing the recording only by copying the signal.

The same goes for films. 

!Broadcasts

Little guidence for copying a broadcast however following the sound recording rationale it would follow that the same would apply in that context.

!Typographical Arrangements

The scope is narrow, because it must be a facsimile copy of the arrangement. (CDPA S.17(5)) Retyping the work in a different font would be sufficient to not constitute reprroduction.

DISTRIBUTION RIGHT

!Distribution

The owner of copyright is the person with the right to issue copies of their work. Distribution right is for issuing of each copy including the original. This is a right to put each copy into commercial circulation.

S.18 refers to issuing copies to the public. Lines up with Article 6 of the WIPO treaty. This is comparatively narrow and encompasses the first act of putting something into circulation, rather than subsequent distribution sale hiring or loan of copies put into circulation already.

CJEU defines distribution broadly as not just launching of copies into circulation but also in preparing to do so. This was found in the CASE of DONNER and the case of DIMENSIONE DIRECT SALES SRI, MICHELE LABIANCA v. KNOLL INTERNATIONAL SPA. 

Though putting things on display was insufficient found in PEEK & CLOPPENBURG SA v. CASSINA SPA

After REULA it is hard to reconcile what this means in light of the statute and its interpretation within the UK.

In the EU infringement does require knowledge in contrast to the UK. It is difficult to tease apart what the state of the law would be in the UK.

!Of copies

Distribution rights applies to tangible copies of work and not electronic copies with the exception of software. 

!To the public

What is the public? A transaction between a wholesaler to a retailer would constitute the public as found in RESPONSE CLOTHING v. THE EDINBURGH WOOLLEN MILL.

!Exhaustion

Part of EU harmonization frameworks. 

Usedsoft GmbH v. Oracle -> CJEU exaustion doctrine can apply to computer programs made available electronically so long as the previous copies are expunged.

Tom Kabinet -> However there was no exhaustion found. Article 4 applied to tangible copies. 

!Modified Objects

Art & Allposters International bv v. Stichting Pictoright -> CJEU the limits of exhaustion for lawfully marketed posters of famous artworks. Where the image was transferred onto canvas. The CJEU found infringement in this case. Exhaustion only applied to the object sold. The transfer to the canvas involved reproduction, and the markets were different and therefore outside of the exhaustion derogation.

!Importation

Distribution right is exhausted by distribution in the EEA. 

Blomqvist v. Rolex SA -> Counterfeit Rolex purchased from a Chinese seller online and was being sent to the customer. Danish authorities intercepted it and destroyed it. CJEU found they were entitled to do so as the distribution infringed copyright.

RENTAL AND LENDING RIGHTS

LDMA, S and F are granted exclusive right to lend copies to the public on the understanding that they will be returned.

PUBLIC PERFORMANCE RIGHTS 

Found in S.19(1) CDPA

Performance of a work in public is an act restricted by copyright in an LDM work. Same for a sound recording, film, or broadcast. No performing right for artistic works. 

Performing the work to the public is the act of infringement.

!Performance

S.19 defined as including the delivery of lectures, addresses, speeches and sermons, as well as any mode of visual or accoustic presentation of work. The public must be present where this performance occurs. 

!Location

Needs to take place where it can be heard or seen.

!In public

Different from S.19(1) and S.19(2)(a) 

In S.19(1) and 19(2)(a)!

A performance in a theatre or somewhere in public would obviously be public. It is held to be increasingly expansive. Places that are open to the public such as hotels and hair salons 

Primary factor is the character of the audience

Jennings v. Stephens -> Section of the general public with no unifying character are public. Performance by members of a women's institute without charge and guests was a performance in public.

In public is in relation to the owner of the copyright. The copyright owner's public.

Duck v. bates -> No infringement through performance of a play by an amateur dramatic club at a hospital since that was for the entertainment of a set unified group.

Errnest Turner Electrical Instruments v. PRS -> Playing music to 600 workers was sufficiently public to find infringement.

Financial considerations also a factor. If it is performed for prorfit then it is likely to be for the public. 

Harms v. Martans -> Infringement by a performance of musical work to the 150 members of a club and 50 guests since there was a subscription and entrance fee paid to the orchestra.

In S.19(2)(b) and (3)!

CJEU test of communication to the public. Applicable to these sections. Strict liability if broadcasts could be heard in public areas, or if it could only be heard because of particular scenarios.

!Who is responsible

Where the performance is live it is obvious who is responsible for the performance. 

PRS v. Hammond's Bradford Brewery co. -> Person who operates the aparatus is responsible or the person actually performing.

COMMUNICATION RIGHT

Applies to LDMA, F, SR, and B. The right to communicate the work to the public. Including the right to broadcast it and the right to make it avaialble. 

ITV Studios v. TV Catch Up -> Rejecting the view that implementation and communication comprised two rights. 

Applies to streaming or making a public boardcast. Rise of communications technology has led to this right being more important than it once was. 

TuneIn v. Warner Music UK -> The UK courts will not depart from the CJEU jurisprudence on communication rights. Though conceding that the CJEU jurisprudence has its own issues.

Communicating!

S.20(2) CDPA communiation includes broadcasting or making available. Includes a lot of activities. TVs in hotel rooms and radio at a rehabilitation centre and hyperlinks too. CJEU justifies it by saying that in order to allow for a high leve; of protection it must be interpreted broadly. 

Broadcast!

CDPA 1988 The right of the copyrirght owner to communicate the work in public includes broadcasting it, transmission of visual images etc.

Making available! 

S.20 CDPA copyright owners have the exclusive right to make the work avaiable to the public by electronic transmission in such a way that members of the public may access it. Includes streaming

Other communications! 

CJEU has a lot of jurisprudence on that. Has five elements.

1. An intervention to give access to or experience the work or other subject matter

2. Going beyond the mere provision of physical facilities.

3. To a public that was not present where hte subject matter originated

4. Would not have access otherwise

5. A deliberate act carried out by a person with full knowledge of the sonsequences

Intervention condition!

User who's intervention gives access to a work that recipients would not otherwise enjoy. Broader than electronic transmission under S.20

There is an act of communication where a broadcast of a sound recording is played. 

Svensson v. Retriever Sveige AB -> Where a broadcast of a sound recording is played.

This includes hyperlinking or selling a device loaded with links to a streaming service.

More than mere physical facilities!

STIM SAMI v. Fleetmanager Sweden AB Nordisk Biluthyrning AB -> Merely supplying TVs to a hotel would not meet the threshold.

S.19(2)(b) and 19(3) could apply for public performance.

Stichting Brein v. Jack Frederik Wullems -> Selling a device loaded with links for streaming services.

EMI Records v. British Sky Broadcasting -> website that indexed bittorrent files to enable users to access them. Held to be public performance.

Public not present at origination!

Intention Requirement!

SGAE v. Rafael Hotels LS -> CJEU referred to the fact that D the hotel proprieter relayed signals that it recieved to hotel rooms as communication to the public because it is the organisation which intervenes in full knowledge of the consequences of its action. 

FAPL -> CJEU treated showing of broadccasts of football matches in pubs to be communication to the public.

Mircom v. Telenet -> Users of bit-torrent, holding that such users would only be liable for communications where they have subscribed to that software giving their consent to its application after having been informed of its characteristics.

These decisions need to be contrasted with CA in TuneIn v. Warner -> Foreign radio services streamed sound recordings, in many cases with the authority of the copyright owners and in line with local law. The services were then aggregated by a third party TuneIn, which redirected into the UK a territory where the permissions had not been sought. Holding that it aws communication to the public, CA indicated that the radio stations were themselves liable for enfringement at PARA 191. This imposed strict liability on foreign radio stations for the fact their content was redirected into the UK without authorization and in conflict with the knowledge/intention requirement in the EU.

GS Media case -> Knowledge of the illegal status of the works being made accessible is important for liability.

Wullems -> Must be knowledge of that specific act.

OR

Ziggo BV -> Through general knowledge that this activity is infringing.

Wullems -> Sale of a device preloaded with copyright infringing content in full knowledge of that content was enough to establish liability.

Pirate bay -> CJEU operators could not be unaware that the platform was used to provide access without theh consent of rightsholders and therefore infringing.

To the Public!

Three core considerations

1. Size of the group, there is a de minimis threshold

2. The character of the group, it must not be a private group

3. The character of the communication was it made with a view to making a profit, and if the consumers were targeted or present by chance.

OSA Case C-351 -> Public included patients in a rehabilitation centre. 

Blue Air Aviation -> Also to passengers on an aircraft, post brexit but decided under S.19(2)(b) these were to the public

But! 

Societa Consortile -> Dental surgery not public and this is because the number of patients was small, stable and relatively pre-determined and the activity was not a profit making technique for attracting business.

Reha Training -> Societa Consortile should be considered in relation to its specific facts and should not be brought outside of it.

BY v. CX -> Court documents to those related to a trial is also not to the public, small group of professionals and therefore not public. This is not affected by the fact that these documents are subsequently avaialbe to the public.

Publishing v. Cruiseco -> Nugee J, accessibility of infringing advertisement to 257 travel agents was not to an indeterminate number of recipients and therefore not to the public. SEE PARA 41

Tom Kabinet -> Resale of E-books CJEU held that distribution right only applied to physical copies and sale could not be exhausted by the sale of an e-book. Communication from one user to another would in all cases infringe the communication right. A communication to a book club would be to the public because anyone could join it. However! Because the software had no limitation that it could be to one person at a time. It was public, might be different if otherwise and might not be communication to the public.

'A new public,'! 

Communication to the public may not be infringement if the work had already been commjunicated to the public with the rightsholders consent.

SGAE -> New public genesis, author llicesned a live broadcast, they could object to the retransmission in hotel rooms. Since the hotel rooms were an addtional service motivated by profit making. 

Further transmission beyond the new public may be infringing.

SGAE -> Expectation the new public was people with television sets and their families. So transmission to a hotel room was outside of that new public. So in effect the public must be new and not a public that has been broadcasted to before.

Tom Kabinet -> Communication of work to a new public since the first user accepted the EULA and limited their use to just one device.

TuneIn -> CA inferred that a right holder who permitted use of material in one country had not contemplated linking to that station globally. Communicating those works to the world and therefore creating a new public.

Hyperlinking!

CJEU has used new public to regulate hyperlinks. Communicating the work to which the link led. However this does not apply to every single hyperlink.

Two clear cases are Svensson and GS Media separate cases.

Hyperlinking is an act of communication so is it a communication to a new public?

Link points to unathorized content - GS Media, then next question is 'Knowledge of Unauthorized Source,' where crucial is if it is done for profit, if profit then construed presumption. If not for profit, no presumption of knowledge unless linker notified of status, if No then no copyright - Svennson, if notified and removed then no copyright, if no then infringement. If presumption rebutted then no copyright infringement.

Link points to authorized content, then, content freely accessible Svensson copyright irrelevant, implied consent Soulier. If there are access restrictions and the link circumvents the restrictions then hyperlinking would be infringement, if not then no infringement.

Who makes a communication! 

Broadcast!

S.6 specifies that the person who makes a broadcast is either the transmitter, or the person providing the item for transmission and makes arrangements with the transmitter to transmit that specific work. 

Other communications!

Mostly clear, it is the uploader. 

Traditionally UK copyright law has differentiated between primary liability and accessorial liability. For an accessory to be liable they must have procured or induced an act. 

Twentieth Century Fox v. Sky UK -> Birss J, distinction between communication to the public and accessory liability when nconsidering the operator of website popcorn time, which aggregated torrent links. The application was the source of the communication but the providers of popcorn time were liable as accessories jointly with the operators of the host websites. The application procured the user access to the host website and therefore to make the unauthorized communication.

CJEU only has primary liability.

Ziggo BV -> Pirate bay case the CJEU found that through indexing that constituted communication and therefore primary liability was found. 

Newzbin case -> UK similar approach to pirate bay.

Peterson v. Youtube -> UK case post Brexit STRONGEST AUTHORITY. Recording of a performer.

Elsevier v. Cyando -> UK case post Brexit STRONGEST AUTHORITY. File hosting service having a medical textbook gray's anatomy. 

Both cases if aware of illegal content then duty to get rid of it.

The fact that the operator knows in a general sense that content is made avaiable illegally on their platform is not sufficient ground to conclude that there is infringement.

Location of the act!

Broadcast location

Hard to ascertain where an act of communication takes place. S.6 of the act, place of broadcasting as where the programme is introduced to. 

Communications other than broadcast location

Football Dataco v. Sportradar -> question arose as to where reutilization occured. D uploaded a collection of sports results in Germany onto a server in the Netherlands from whichc the results were accessible in the UK. Action brought in English court. CJEU ruled that the wrongful act takes place in MS when the person performing the act intends to target members of that MS.

Lilley v. Chartered Institute of Management Accountants -> Applied this targetting standard to the UK.

TuneIn v. Warner -> CA decided potential liability of radio service which collected and organized hyperlinks of 100,000 radiostations which were operating abroad. The court referred to the targetting test. 10% of audience came from the UK. If TuneIn targets the UK then the original broadcasters must also as consequence. 

Potential for reform.

ADAPTIVE RIGHTS

S.21 confers the owner of copyright the exclusive right to make an adaptation of an LDM work. Not granted to A, and SR and F. 

Adaptation is defined differently in relation to the specific work. 

Norozian v. Arks -> Dramatization of a work into film. 

Adaptation means translation essentially

AUTHORIZATION RIGHTS

Right to authorize others to do any of the restricted acts. CDPA S.16(2) also granting a right for a third person to do an act, see CBS v. Amstrad. 

CBS v. Amstrad -> HoL used the term authorize restrictively. Amstrad manufactured and marketed a double speed twin tape recorder. Boasted how the model now features hi speed dubbing to oenable recordings from one casette to another and to make a copy of your favourite casette. Asterisk said dthat this was potentially infringement. HoL said that neither the sale of equipment nor advertizement was an authorization. HoL held there was no authorization because it was up to the authority and Amstrad did not purport to have the authority.

Twentieth Century Fox v. Newzbin -> Films that third parties had uploaded to usenet sites without license. Newzbin employed editors to index these sites and offered a paid service to access the index. Kitchin J explained that while authorization required a grant or purported grant, this could be implied through circumstances. Question is would a reasonable person believe in the circumstances that the granter was authorizing copyright. Found in that case.

There are various activities such as the selling of a preloaded device with infringing material which would infringe this authorization right.
