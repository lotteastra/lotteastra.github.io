REPRODUCTIVE RIGHTS

Copying

S.17 confers the oldest rights. Infringement takes place regardless of the duration or method. So long as a copy is made.

Technische Universität Darmstadt v. Eugen Ulmer KG -> Demonstrated that storing information on a USB was sufficientn to constitute an infringement of the right to copy.

Getty v. Stability AI -> Also applies with regard to putting information onto a cloud service.

R v. Higgs -> Equally accessing material online where a copy is only stored in the RAM is equally sufficient.

There is an argument that copyright should only protect work as a communication, and therefore the copy would need to be distributed in order to be infringement.

!LDMA works

Statutory Definition under S.17(2) and includes other methods of production. So scanning in a book would not be the same way but it would still constitute copying. 

Francis Day & Hunter v. Bron -> The works must be objectively similar. 

Davis (J & S) Holdings v. Wright Health Group -> Using the instructions to make something would not be copyright infringement even if it used a copyrighted literary work as the recipe. 

Sandman v. Panasonic -> Circuit diagram used to make the electronics described however may be infringement. 

Autospin (Oil Seals) v. Beehive Spinning -> Drawing a scene described in a novel would not be infringement on account of the fidelity however.

!Sound Recordings and Films

Reproduction for films and sound recordings is narrower than for LDMA works. 

R. Arnold 'Content Copyrights and Signal COpyrights: The case for a Rational Scheme of Protection,' (2011) 1 QMJIP 272 -> The protection protects the signal and not the content.

Creation Records v. News Group Newspapers -> Not the case with photographs where the copyright extends to the content and not only the specific recording.

As such copyright in a sound recording is not infringed by someone transcribing the recording only by copying the signal.

The same goes for films. 

!Broadcasts

Little guidence for copying a broadcast however following the sound recording rationale it would follow that the same would apply in that context.

!Typographical Arrangements

The scope is narrow, because it must be a facsimile copy of the arrangement. (CDPA S.17(5)) Retyping the work in a different font would be sufficient to not constitute reprroduction.

DISTRIBUTION RIGHT

!Distribution

The owner of copyright is the person with the right to issue copies of their work. Distribution right is for issuing of each copy including the original. This is a right to put each copy into commercial circulation.

S.18 refers to issuing copies to the public. Lines up with Article 6 of the WIPO treaty. This is comparatively narrow and encompasses the first act of putting something into circulation, rather than subsequent distribution sale hiring or loan of copies put into circulation already.

CJEU defines distribution broadly as not just launching of copies into circulation but also in preparing to do so. This was found in the CASE of DONNER and the case of DIMENSIONE DIRECT SALES SRI, MICHELE LABIANCA v. KNOLL INTERNATIONAL SPA. 

Though putting things on display was insufficient found in PEEK & CLOPPENBURG SA v. CASSINA SPA

After REULA it is hard to reconcile what this means in light of the statute and its interpretation within the UK.

In the EU infringement does require knowledge in contrast to the UK. It is difficult to tease apart what the state of the law would be in the UK.

!Of copies

Distribution rights applies to tangible copies of work and not electronic copies with the exception of software. 

!To the public

What is the public? A transaction between a wholesaler to a retailer would constitute the public as found in RESPONSE CLOTHING v. THE EDINBURGH WOOLLEN MILL.

!Exhaustion

Part of EU harmonization frameworks. 

Usedsoft GmbH v. Oracle -> CJEU exaustion doctrine can apply to computer programs made available electronically so long as the previous copies are expunged.

Tom Kabinet -> However there was no exhaustion found. Article 4 applied to tangible copies. 

!Modified Objects

Art & Allposters International bv v. Stichting Pictoright -> CJEU the limits of exhaustion for lawfully marketed posters of famous artworks. Where the image was transferred onto canvas. The CJEU found infringement in this case. Exhaustion only applied to the object sold. The transfer to the canvas involved reproduction, and the markets were different and therefore outside of the exhaustion derogation.

!Importation

Distribution right is exhausted by distribution in the EEA. 

Blomqvist v. Rolex SA -> Counterfeit Rolex purchased from a Chinese seller online and was being sent to the customer. Danish authorities intercepted it and destroyed it. CJEU found they were entitled to do so as the distribution infringed copyright.

RENTAL AND LENDING RIGHTS

LDMA, S and F are granted exclusive right to lend copies to the public on the understanding that they will be returned.

PUBLIC PERFORMANCE RIGHTS 

Found in S.19(1) CDPA

Performance of a work in public is an act restricted by copyright in an LDM work. Same for a sound recording, film, or broadcast. No performing right for artistic works. 

Performing the work to the public is the act of infringement.

!Performance

S.19 defined as including the delivery of lectures, addresses, speeches and sermons, as well as any mode of visual or accoustic presentation of work. The public must be present where this performance occurs. 

!Location

Needs to take place where it can be heard or seen.

!In public

Different from S.19(1) and S.19(2)(a) 

In S.19(1) and 19(2)(a)!

A performance in a theatre or somewhere in public would obviously be public. It is held to be increasingly expansive. Places that are open to the public such as hotels and hair salons 

Primary factor is the character of the audience

Jennings v. Stephens -> Section of the general public with no unifying character are public. Performance by members of a women's institute without charge and guests was a performance in public.

In public is in relation to the owner of the copyright. The copyright owner's public.

Duck v. bates -> No infringement through performance of a play by an amateur dramatic club at a hospital since that was for the entertainment of a set unified group.

Errnest Turner Electrical Instruments v. PRS -> Playing music to 600 workers was sufficiently public to find infringement.

Financial considerations also a factor. If it is performed for prorfit then it is likely to be for the public. 

Harms v. Martans -> Infringement by a performance of musical work to the 150 members of a club and 50 guests since there was a subscription and entrance fee paid to the orchestra.

In S.19(2)(b) and (3)!

CJEU test of communication to the public. Applicable to these sections. Strict liability if broadcasts could be heard in public areas, or if it could only be heard because of particular scenarios.

!Who is responsible

Where the performance is live it is obvious who is responsible for the performance. 

PRS v. Hammond's Bradford Brewery co. -> Person who operates the aparatus is responsible or the person actually performing.

COMMUNICATION RIGHT

Applies to LDMA, F, SR, and B. The right to communicate the work to the public. Including the right to broadcast it and the right to make it avaialble. 

ITV Studios v. TV Catch Up -> Rejecting the view that implementation and communication comprised two rights. 

Applies to streaming or making a public boardcast. Rise of communications technology has led to this right being more important than it once was. 

TuneIn v. Warner Music UK -> The UK courts will not depart from the CJEU jurisprudence on communication rights. Though conceding that the CJEU jurisprudence has its own issues.

Communicating!

S.20(2) CDPA communiation includes broadcasting or making available. Includes a lot of activities. TVs in hotel rooms and radio at a rehabilitation centre and hyperlinks too. CJEU justifies it by saying that in order to allow for a high leve; of protection it must be interpreted broadly. 

Broadcast!

CDPA 1988 The right of the copyrirght owner to communicate the work in public includes broadcasting it, transmission of visual images etc.

Making available! 

S.20 CDPA copyright owners have the exclusive right to make the work avaiable to the public by electronic transmission in such a way that members of the public may access it. Includes streaming

Other communications! 

CJEU has a lot of jurisprudence on that. Has five elements.

1. An intervention to give access to or experience the work or other subject matter

2. Going beyond the mere provision of physical facilities.

3. To a public that was not present where hte subject matter originated

4. Would not have access otherwise

5. A deliberate act carried out by a person with full knowledge of the sonsequences

Intervention condition!

User who's intervention gives access to a work that recipients would not otherwise enjoy. Broader than electronic transmission under S.20

There is an act of communication where a broadcast of a sound recording is played. 

Svensson v. Retriever Sveige AB -> Where a broadcast of a sound recording is played.

This includes hyperlinking or selling a device loaded with links to a streaming service.

More than mere physical facilities!

STIM SAMI v. Fleetmanager Sweden AB Nordisk Biluthyrning AB -> Merely supplying TVs to a hotel would not meet the threshold.
