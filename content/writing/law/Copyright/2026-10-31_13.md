title: Copyright Law Notes Authorship
---

Author is defined in the CDPA as the person who creates the work. There are special provisions for where there is however more than one author. 

Though there are few issues in ascertaining the author of an ldma work in practice. Entrepenurial works are where the problems arise themselves. 

Author is a legal fiction for the creation of rights. Person by whom the arrangements necessary for the creation is undertaken. 

S.104 LDMA works authoriship. S.105 for sound, films and computer programs. The burden of proof lies on the person aside from the named author to prove that they were the actual author. 

The author of an artistic work is the creator for an LDMA work. In S.3(2) the author may not be the person who fixes the work however.

Contribution is important. Someone who copies won't be an author. 

Donoghue v. Allied Newspapers -> Farwell J !DOWN!

"A mere amanuensis does not, by taking down word for word the language of the author, become in any sense the owner of the copyright. That is the property of the author. I think the explanation of that is this, that in which the copyright exists is the particular form of language by which is conveyed the information which is to be conveyed. If the idea, however brilliant and however clever it may be, is nothing more than an idea, and is not put into any form of words, or any form of expression such as a picture or a play, then there is no such thing as copyright at all."

"nevertheless the particular form of language in which those adventures or stories were conveyed to the public was the language of Mr. Felstead, and not the language of Mr. Donoghue."

Cummins v. Bond -> Spiritualist who was copying writing dictated from beyond the grave could hold copyright for the skill in translating spiritual communications into archaic English and as such was the author. 

Where the contributions for the work are intellectual in nature they will not be able to form any part of co-ownership, rather the work should be part of the actual process of reproduction. 

In works with unknown authorship then the first fixation will be the author and the owner of the copyright, and the copyright date should be 70 years from the date on which it was made available for LDMA works.

S.9(2) CPDA defines who is the author of entrepenurial works. 

S.3(2)(aa) CDPA -> Producer is defined there.

Sound recordsing the producer, the person by whom the arrangmeents necessary for the making of the sound recording are undertaken. This may be the record company or someone else. 

Act amended in 1994 so that it was not the film producer but the director who was the author. 

Slater v. Wimmer -> Defines who the director is. The person who had creative control over the making of the film. Though in that case it was the cameraman.

Depends on the contract generally.

The author of a typographical arrangement is the publisher. CDPA S.9(2)(a)

Martin v. Kogan -> Case about jiont ownership. CA pointed out four elements. Collaboration, authorship, contribution, non-distinctness of contribution.

COLLABORATION

The work must be produced through collaboration of the authors. There must be some common design or plan that the authors work together towards. 

Martin v. Kogan Para 32, 53

There must be meetings between the authors. In Martin v. Kogan they met over skype [para 136] and that was sufficient, as well as working together in person.

IDDQD v. Codeberry -> Collaboration could occur by those involved updating the postcode address database, collaboration in the assembly of data. So adding to the same database would be collaboration.

AUTHORSHIP

Not the person who pushes the pen according to Martin v. Kogan. In Kogan their contributions were what the character would sing, and the way the voice coach would speak to the character in the film. The court highlighted this as being authorship. It must be an authorial contribution ie, the same kind of thing the author was doing.

CONTRIBUTION

Necessary to show that each of the authors contributed to the work in order to make them a joint author and their contribution must be original. Part of their own AOIC. 

Earlier case law held that the contribution had to be significant as well as original. In Martin v. Corgan it is substantial.

‘joint authors must be authors, in the sense that they must have contributed a significant amount of the skill which went into the creation of the work.'

Para 53

There is a potential conflict with Infopaq where substantiality and significance are not relevant.

Martin v. Kogan -> 20% of the copyright based on the contribution in that case. 

NON DISTINCTNESS

IDDQD v. Codeberry -> Where the contributions cannot be separated then joint authorship will be considered. 

Martin v. Kogan -> So long as parts of the script can be identified even though they are non-distinct from the whole then some copyright will be granted over those parts. 

FIRST OWNERSHIP

Generally an author will be the first owner of copyright. CDPA S.11(1)

However this is not the case for employees

S.11(2)

Crown copyright is also an exceptoin. The author can also assign their copyright to third parties, so by assignment then the copyright would be changed. Though the duration is still contingent on first author. 

S.3(2) Where an LDMA work is made in the course of employment then the employer is the first owner while the employee retains moral rights. 

Employee is defined in the 1988 Act CDPA S.178 as anyone under a contract of service, distinguished from a contract for services. 

It must be made in the course of employment. 

Stevenson Jordan v. MacDonald and Evans -> Accountant or his employer owned copyright for a series of lectures the accountant had made about budgetary control for businesses. Since the employer had paid the epxenses. However since the employee had not been ordered then the copyright belonged to the employee and not the employer. 

Noah v. Shuba -> Wrote a guide to skin piercing and he was employed as an epidemiologist. Since the draft was written at home it was outside the scope of employment and he was under no contract to produce books. 

Missing Link Software v. Magee -> Even though software was written outside of work time and on the employees own equipment since he was contracted to produce software then the copyright laid with the employer as part of the course of his employment. 

Agreements to the contrary -> Agreements to the contrary may be oral, express or implied. 

Under CDPA copyright in a commissioned work belongs to the author of the commissioned work. S.85 CDPA for films.

R. Griggs Group v. Evans -> Griggs distributors of boots commissioned an ad agency Jordan to produce a logo. Evans did freelance work for Jordan and produced the logo and was paid. Nothing was said of copyright. Agreement that the copyright had transferred to the commissioner was implied in that case. Para 57

This conflicts with the statute and can be seen as undermining the power between the two to merely have the copyright be transferred like that.
