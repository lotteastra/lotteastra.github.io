---
title: On Justifications for Copyright
---

What follows is not an argument I necessarily agree with but an argument I will formulate for the sake of enjoyment. 

This justification draws heavily from Frye's essay 'Literary Landlords,' within which the author breaks down justifications for copyright by looking at them as what they actually are, landlords seeking rent. In essence copyright is the collision of a moral justification used to cloak the extraction of rent. There is one metaphor used which relates to housing. I think I would expand that metaphor here by synthesising Frye's theory with psychology. 

In particular the way Frye talks about the housing market's need to exist being predicated on people's need for housing. Copyright law operates in the same way with people's need to be less bored, which can take the form of creation or consumption. Copyright law can then be understood in terms of literature as being an author's attempt to meet this need. Therefore copyright is an attempt to gain profit from this specific need, or the need in human society for ideas. As such copyright should be limited because often the existence of copyright is only incidental in the creation of these works, and the more works that are open to be openly iterated on by more people would allow for even more creation overall and this need to be met more completely and aptly. As such the trend should be for copyright to be reduced so that this human need for ideas is better served.
