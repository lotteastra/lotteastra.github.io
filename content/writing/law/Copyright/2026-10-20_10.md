Question One

The Great Narrato is an impromptu story-teller. In the summer months, he performs at “alternative” festivals, telling improvised stories from his imagination. He is an advocate of “real” art and forbids his audience from recording his stories. Last year, he performed at the “Fire over the Water” festival, at which he told a long, rambling story about a character called “Riverstick”. On that occasion, Zara was in the audience.  Some months after the event, Zara wrote a sequel to The Great Narrato’s Riverstick story, which she had remembered in considerable detail as well as using a generative-AI programme to support some parts of her draft.

Could copyright subsist in: 
a. The Great Narrato’s Riverstick story, or any elements of that story, and/or 

Must be sufficiently connected to the UK in order to be protected here, so that is a preliminary matter. The subject matter must be sufficiently identifiable in order to be protected, and must be the author's own intellectual creation. The AI angle also questions who is the person who would hold the copyright.

The operative case on subject matter being sufficiently identifiable is Levola, the cream cheese case, and in this case it's an issue because what element is owned by Z is in itself questionable, can it be identified separately from GN's own work, and if it can, then it would follow it could be protected.

In this case there is a fixation, in her writing, but is this writing original? It must not be copied though given that she is doing a sequel then that part could be severed away. 

THJ Systems v. Sheridan -> Affirmed that Author's Own Intellectual Creation is the law of the land in the UK. Paragraph 23. Author's own intellectual creation is how originality is interpreted here. 

Cofomel -> 'subject matter reflects the personality of its author as an expression of his free and creative choices,' 

CJEU has given 4 broad elements

1. Expression

2. Author must be able to make free choices

3. Author must be able to make creative choices

4. The creator must have stamped their personality on the work their 'personal touch,' 

So did Z engage in expression? Yes she did, her choices were free, and she did make creative choices, but personal touch is more in question because she was writing a sequel to another story. Though the threshold is quite low as shown in the Nottinghill case. 

For the AI element there is a case in the UK that indicates that instead of being the model maker it wouldd be the prompter who would get copyright in this case. 

Also character is at issue see the Delboy case. Would be infringement for the character though this was decided pre-REULR and while the question gives no indication as if this took place pre or post, if post then it is not a given that the character would be protected. 

See also the Lidl case for if something is too basic. 

b. Zara’s story? Would it make any difference to your answer if, unknown to anyone else, Sam had recorded The Great Narrato’s performance at the “Fire over the Water” festival on her phone? Would copyright subsist in Sam’s recording?

It would create more issues with originality since elements would have been directly copied and it would have been hard for the creative work to be sufficiently identifiable seperate from GN's work, however this was probably not insurrmountable. 

For the recording the recording would act as a fixation but it would be owned by GN and not S as S engaged in no originality.

a	a new, pre-planned “set-piece”, practised and performed by a football team in a competitive match;

Yes as a dramatic work. 

b	a perfect forgery of a 17th Century oil painting;

No as it's not original. 

c	a photograph of a mid-20th Century sofa in an auction catalogue;

If purely functional then not able to reach the threshold of AOIC

d	Richard Prince’s artworks, discussed in this video clip:

[NB – (1) the speakers are discussing US law and (2) we are interested only in the question whether Prince’s artworks have copyright and not in the question of whether Prince has infringed the speakers’ copyright];

e	a conversation between two friends;

If intended merely to convey information then there are no creative choices and for want of creative choices then no copyright. Also lack of fixation.

Instructions protected as literary work but not the design of the shelves.
