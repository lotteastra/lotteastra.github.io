title: Copyright Notes Justifications
---

Copyright law is justified in various ways though there are three primary types of theory. 

1. Moral justifications, derived from philosophy either John Locke's labour mixing theory, or Hegel and creative works being an expression of the author's personality and therefore they have a moral obligation to own those specific works on account of the fact that those works are a part of them or an extension of them. (See also Kant's speech based justification, where you own the speech you say is owned by you as you are the only person with authority over that speech, though different from the overall natural right because someone else may use your speech in order to express themselves effectively and there shouldn't be an impediment to that.)

2. Rewards/Incentive justifications, while rewards and incentives seem similar, reward conceptions frame copyright as a reward in themselves, not a reward intended to bring out a specific result. Incentive structures deem that creative works are valuable in themselves and therefore they should have protection since there is a market failure without copyright law existing. Copying a work is easier than making a new work from scratch and therefore the incentives without copyright lay far more in copying than innovating, and copyright exists to protect from this market failure and incentivize new works being made. 

3. Neoliberal economic theory, justifies copyright on the basis of the tragedy of the commons holding that if works are freely distributed then it would follow that those works would soon become overexploited, and thus private ownership in perpetuity is necessary in order to protect the work from being overexploited. 

Copyright law in the UK is also subject to multiple international instruments, and EU legislation. 

The Berne Convention -> First development of international copyright, protects authorial works but only literary and artistic works, removes formalities and creates limited exceptions for states to depart from the convention. The Berne convention gives a three step test for these depatures from the norms. 

1. The exceptions must be limited to certain special cases. 
2. The exceptions must not conflict with normal exploitation of the work. 
3. The exceptions must not unreasonably prejudice the legitimate interests of the author. 

Signatories to the Berne Convention in doing so become Members of the Berne Union, and they also pledge to give authors rather than copyright owners the moral rights of attribution and integrity. 

The Rome Convention -> Followed Berne and expanded it to soundtracks, performers and other rights related to movies in particular. 

TRIPS -> Expanded the Berne Convention and applied it to computer programs which are counted as literary works and to compilations of data which may by the arrangement or selection 'constitute intellectural creations.' Also made disputes part of the WTO. 

WIPO -> Repeats the extensions in TRIPS. Includes and expands copyright to more digital frontiers. 

WIPO Performances and Phonograms Treaty -> Gave more rights to performers and soundtrack authors. Given moral rights of attribution. 

Marrakesh Treaty for the Visually Impaired -> Gave limited copyright exceptions for blind people. 

Beijing Treaty -> Still in the process of ratification, gave more rights to audiovisual performances and expanding from 20 years in Rome to 50 years from where the performance was made. 

EUROPE

Europe harmonized areas related to new tehcnologies. There were a series of directives from 1991 to 2001 related to software, cable, and satellite broadcasting. 2001 Information Society Directive being an important step  on that.
