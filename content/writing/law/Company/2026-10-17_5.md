Question 2:

Mega Mine Resources plc (“MMR”) is the English parent company of a mining group. Its wholly-owned subsidiary, Super Minerals Ltd (“SML”), operates a copper mine and a dam near the town of Kasanda, Uganda. SML has its own board of directors, but two MMR executives are also on its board.

MMR’s annual report describes the group as ‘committed to industry-leading environmental performance’. It published a Group Sustainability Framework, where it ‘expects each subsidiary to maintain standards at least equivalent to those set out’ in the document, and that ‘local management is responsible for its implementation’. MMR employs a group Sustainability Adviser, Ms Ssemakula, who visits sites on request and circulates guidance notes to subsidiaries. She has no authority to direct managers, but she is very influential due to her world-class expertise. For example, when she raised concerns about waste storage by another subsidiary in 2019, that subsidiary suspended its practices and asked her to advise
on a replacement regime, which was adopted in full.

In 2025, SML’s own consultants reported that the Kasanda dam required repairs. Ms Ssemakula also raised serious concerns while providing detailed recommendations for repairs and maintenance. SML sought funding under the group treasury arrangement. MMR declined this, as the works were ‘not commercially justified at current prices’. The dam failed in 2026, which destroyed the farmland, houses and water supplies at Kasanda. 100 SML employees also suffered severe injuries during the clean-up, as no protective
equipment was given to them. SML is now insolvent.

Advise the Kasanda residents and SML’s employees on MMR’s liability
