OSTENSIBLE AUTHORITY

Classic statement by Diplock LJ in Freeman & Lockyer v. Buckhurs Park Properties Mangal ltd -> Actual authority may be express or implied, apparent authority arises from a representation of authority on which a third party relies. 

1. Representation: The third party was given a representation that the agent had authoirty to enter into that kind of contract on the company's behalf.

2. Authorised source: The representation was made by someone with actual authority to manage the company's business generally or the matters to which the contract is related.

3. Reliance: The third party relied on that representation when entering into the contract.

Representation may arise through conduct.

Actual authority arises through consent. Apparent authority arises through a representation by the company made to a third party.

Actual authority is express or implied through a board resolution or an office held or the course of conduct (Hely-Hutchinson). Apparent authority is created by holding out by someone with actual authority usually the board (Freeman ^ Lockyer).

Actual authority the third party's position is irrelevant it binds even if the third party is unaware of the authority. Apparent authority the third party must rely on the representation it fails if they were on notice or put on inquiry.

Actual authority the legal basis is the agent acts within their mandate. Apparent authority the legal basis is estoppel.

Outcomes are the same for both, the company is bound to the third party provided the conditions are met.
