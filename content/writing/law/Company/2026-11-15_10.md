Theories of Corporate Governance. 

What is a company? 

Invented by lawyers, industrial revolution needed infrastructure and capital therefore. How is it conceptualized now.

Two kinds of conception of corporation

Real Entity theory

Aggregation of individuals

Corporate governance focusses on aggregation of individualism and nexus of contracts. A real identity with a company sits behind the individuals of a company. 

Chandler -> Traditional enterprise model shifts to industrial corporations and those functions are seprated. Shareholding from management. Managers lack the expertise etc.

Ownership and control rhetoric is crucial. 

Chandler attributes that specialized management was able to expand industrial capacity because it unites capital and labour. Economies of scope. Efficiencies and advantages of growth in compensation for this separation of ownership and control. 

Contractual model concentrates on relationships between key players in the company. 

Relationships are akin to contracts being vehicles for exchange (Jensen and Mecking)

Identifies a contract between shareholders and managers. Shareholders are principles and managers are agents. Part of agency theory.

An agency problem arises though managers might prefer their own position annd rent seek. Alternatively they might fail in the level of competence expected of them. Shareholders might find it difficult to actually monitor managers. 

Adam Smith -> Thinking about joint stock companies being potentially inefficient as the managers might not have the 'anxious vigilance,' that an owner would have in that sense. There can be a conflict of interests.

Berle and Means -> Classic foundational corporate governance text for identifying that problem. Adolf Berle undertook an empirical study of how companies were organized. Shareholding body was largely dispersed. Tries to identify governance problems. Position of owner is a legal interest while the managers have actual control and the interests of control might be opposed to those of management. Managers might have a lot of discretion if shareholding is dispersed. 

John Maynard Keyenes -> Wrotes about the large modern corporation and the separation and the establishment of the company as more of a public actor. 

Rathenau -> Empahsized the separation as the de-individiaulization of ownership which makes a company separate which makes the company more autonomous. 

Berle Dodd debate -> Text in who's interests is the company run. Debate that took place in law journals. Shareholders lack effective control and there's an accountability deficit. Who should directors be accountable to. In the context of the great depression. 

Berle wrote speeches for FDR

Berle proposed that public policy should define a fiduciary duty for management and in the interest of shareholders. Trustees for shareholders. There needs to be clear accountability so there isn't deficit. 

Dodd replied in favour of public policy safeguarding multi consistency and community responsibilities. Dodds saw it as a collective entity and sees it as an opportunity for endowing companies with broader responsibilities and capacity. They can take on more of a public role with social responsibilities. In line with Keynes and Rathenau.

The authors are not that far apart in the debate and in subsequent years they change their positions but they have been broadly authoritative. 

Mix of solutions became in vogue. Post WW2 management were more long termist because of the separation and that gave management more power and discretion. Director's duties and general meetings but shareholders were too numerous and proxy voting meant there wasn't a lot of intervention. The managerialist era.

Berle contractualism, proposed that the managerial company would work were the shareholder loses their sense of entitlement. 

Managerialist era a lot of power sat with the board of directors. 

Contractual model and agency problems. From the 1960s these solutions to the agency problem don't change but different ways of thinking arise. Not just legal controls or regulation and think about it in terms of incentives and property and the context in which companies operate. 

Big changes in financial markets and shareholding. Pension trusts and shareholding being different than before and the politics of the markets and shareholding in general. 

Corporate governance and markets. What gets emphasized is the organizational nature of the company. They look in an empirical way at the different individual actors within a company. Market context in which companies operate. Place of the company within markets. If there are problems in a company selling the shares might make the company more vulnerable to takeover.

Worker engagement used to be another actor within corporations. 

Polarization between those who wanted more state regulation responding to corporate governance failures, and law/economics perspective. 

Meckling -> Looking at the conflicts that exist within corporations. Lots of individuals with their own self interests. Try bring the various interests into alignment together. 

Performance related pay with share options, market for corporate control, shareholder voting rights, directors duties, corporate codes and independent directors are all different solutions within the contractual model. 

Friedman -> More normative conception 

Contractual model

Shareholders draft the legal documents and a company is born, shareholders are the most important stakeholder, shareholder primacy. Though a business needs employees and they are not part of the company they are expendable. Company is focussed on profit making and that is key. 

Law/economics model can have rejections to it

Automatic Self-Cleansing Filter Synidicate -> Collins MR opined that a simple majority of shareholders is not the principal able to alter the mandate of the directors

Gramaphone & TYpewriter co. ltd v. Stanley -> Directors are not servants to obey directions given by the shareholders as individuals. They are not agentns appointed by and bound to serve the shareholders as their principals they are persons who may by the articles be entrusted with control of the business. 

Courts have rejected that directors are mere agents in the first place. 

There are contentions about it. Narrow assumptions about human behaviour, self interest rather than co-operation. 

Assumptions of the market for corporate control, takeovers are supposed in the theory lead to more efficient outcomes.

Overly shareholder dominant conception where they have priority over all else. 

Agency problem is misdiagnosed as shareholders introduces different problems and might be more short-termist, and giving too much money back to shareholders. 

Alternate theories which exist in response

Blair and Stout -> Contractarian theory -> Company is an economic actor and productive activity requires co-ordination between two or more people. Wider base of accountability proposed. Primary job of company board is not to acct as agents, but trustees for the company itself.
