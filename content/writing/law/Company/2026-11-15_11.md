Question 1:
Please find, read and be prepared to discuss the Berle and Dodd debate, noted above in the reading list.

Dodd:

Quotes young 'trustees,' notion

Idea that if the company is a real entity and the real entity has some social responsibilities then the only way to do that is by affording legal freedom to the managers themselves rather than waiting for the 'unanimous,' consent of the shareholders. 

Given the changing attitudes towards the corporation where it is supposed to embody some kind of social responsibility then it is necessary to have a part of the company that is responsible to that end. Having managers pursue shareholder profit only is in conflict with this new set of norms and will lead to the blunt force of increasing regulation. 

In a sense the insistence on shareholder value will create conflict that in turn will undermine public confidence in business.

Berle:

Centres questions of property and protection of it and frames protections of shareholder rights as protections of private property in general. Therefore social responsbility is promoted by protecting property rights since that is what people rely on to sustain themselves. 

My opinion:

I share Dodd's opinion in the sense that I believe that managers are best placed to understand the ultimate needs of the company. They are most in contact with it and with the various people to whom a company will have responsibilities, and relationships which the company needs to trust in.

Berle makes a mistake in reasoning as he approaches stock as being the same as any other property, but this is not true. When you buy a mug you are entitled to drink from it and to gain benefit from it you need not manage it, perhaps you may repair it when necessary but it is mostly a negative function, you buy so that you may access the mug unrestricted. An empowered shareholder by contrast is using their property rights to engage in positive action and introducing this element to property is both unnecessary and inconsistent. Berle did have a stronger case when selling shares was more difficult but in the modern era it is much easier for a given shareholder to sell. 

Further, the power of each individual shareholder is proportionate to their capital investment, but not their expertise or trust within the company. This means that smaller shareholders are effectively barred from having any more control than they would before, functionally empowering shareholders over management means that large shareholders with no check on their power and little contact with the company directly are empowered to do as they please which is to enrich themselves. 

Much is said about managers siphoning profits for themselves but nothing is said about corporate plundering and the degredation of product and asset for the benefit of large shareholders.
