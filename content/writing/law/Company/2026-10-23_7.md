Question 1:
Roy, John and Sarah are the directors of Abbot Ltd, a company that manufactures horseshoes. They also each hold one-third of the shares in the company. The model articles are amended by the following clause:

“All decisions of the board are by majority vote except for votes on transactions with a value greater than £50,000. In such case a decision is only valid if all the directors’ consent to the transaction.”

Roy, John, Sarah and the company are also the only parties to a shareholders’ agreement in which all the parties agree not to increase the share capital of the company without the agreement of all the parties to the shareholders’ agreement.

Roy has recently returned from holiday and found that during his absence there had been a Board meeting, which approved the purchase of a large tract of land for development purposes worth £100,000. The Board also proposes funding the purchase by increasing the share capital of the company.

Roy is very unhappy about these developments and wishes to stop them. Advise
him as to his options.


Quin & Axtens ltd. v. Salmon -> Director's right to veto upheld. Two directors had right to veto some property transactions and they went ahead. The shareholders wanted to go around this veto. The articles of association gave manegerial responsibilities and veto and going around them was going around the articles. Therefore the veto could not be gone around. 

Director has a right to veto based on their management responsibilities which are in the articles of association and therefore would likely be binding and hence Roy could enforce his veto right much like in Quin.

Russell v. Northern Bank Development Corp ltd. -> Agreement not to raise more capital. Lord Jauncy the two dimensions of shareholder agreements those who agreed would be bound under a personal obligation that each had undertaken and was seperate from the company's own obligations. Raising capital is statutory powers and taking away those rights through a shareholders agreement would be fettering. They can be severable so some parts may be valid and some parts may be invalid and they may be severable. Enforceable against them but not the company.

However following Russell the shareholder agreement likely would not be enforceable. So capital could be raised.

Imperial Hydropathic Hotel Case -> Directors were supposed to serve terms and retire by rotation. The Shareholders were unhappy and it was held that this was illegal because it contravened the articles of association, even though they had the power, they had to follow the proceedures and order of operations.

Considering that in Imperial the proper proceedure had to be followed, given that the articles were not amended while he was away then it would follow he would still have the power to veto the transaction.

Question 2:
Why would a shareholder wish to use a shareholders’ agreement?


Enforceable by s/holders against each other; 

Can permit enforcement of outsider rights; 

Does not require court to distinguish between wrongs to co and wrongs to s/holder as members (c.f. Foss v Harbottle); 

Available to deal with sensitive matters, incl. directors’ remuneration and dividend policy, without need for disclosure to Companies House; 

Agreements cannot be altered by simple majority, which ensures that changes cannot be made without consent; 
remedies for breach are available as of right (which is not the case with company law remedies, which typically are discretionary).
