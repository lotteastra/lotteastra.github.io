1. Black and White developed a product to increase efficiency in the machine tooling industry. They have recently been made redundant and wish to set up in a business together. They have some capital, in the form of their redundancy payments, but will need more to establish the business. They seek your advice about the best form of organisation to adopt for their new business.

(a) Advise them as to the best legal form for their situation (do not consider the implications raised in part (b) at this point)

B and W are two people, they could form a company, or they could form a partnership, with limited liability versions of both. In practice however given the small business they would be running any lines of credit they would seek to use would require personal guarantees, rather limiting the usefulness of limited liability. Ultimately a Limited Liability Private Company would be an attractive model, as it would allow for the prestige of adding limited to the end of their name, while also futureproofing for when they are able to access lines of credit without needing to make personal guarantees. A private company reflecting that they are not seeking to raise public funds and it would simplify the incorporation process. A limited liability company would also afford protection in case of death.

(b) Red has a large sum of money available, which would be of considerable
assistance to Black and White. Red is willing to invest this sum in their business, but is not interested in having any active management role. How might Red’s position be catered for?

In this case a private limited company would be the best option as well, as it would allow a split between ownership and management, shares could be issued to R by B and W, without any need for R to act as a director and only excercising control through any Annual meetings though these are not required for a private limited company and that would allow B, W and R the flexibility to operate as they see fit. Particularly given that R wants seemingly little to do with the management. Furthermore the fact that R has little desire to manage the company minimizes the chance of disputes and sidesteps a potential limitation of a private company, that being the tyranny of the majority shareholders. Given that they are not making management decisions there is little opportunity for disputes to arise. 

2. From what you understand of companies so far, what is the function of company law? Discuss.

The function of company law is to promote innovation by minimizing risk for investors and company directors. The amount of liability is limited in most company structures. The Company Acts also create transparency for public companies where a prospectus is regulated and issued, in line with case law, to protect from misrepresentation and facilitate fair dealings. The split in ownership and management enabled by a company also allows for those with capital to utilize it actively in the economy easier. Without such ease then they may be tempted to allow their capital to rot in a savings account rather than allowing it to circulate in the wider economy. In a sense company law exists to marry those with capital with those who want to manage a company, while limiting the liability for both. In doing so it provides an organizational structure for large trading concerns to exist.
