Question 1:
Have the UK corporate governance committees and the Corporate Governance Code been a success?

!ANSWER
MacNeil and Li, "‘Comply or Explain’" (2006) 14 Corporate Governance Evidence that the performance of a company means that the non-compliance is excused making it ineffective when a company appears to be doing well.

Equally real world examples. Nortel, Enron, Carillion, all seemed to comply with Corporate Governance statements and were doing very well, right up until the music stopped. 

"It might be thought to be churlish to be critical of the code approach when it comes to accountability, as it needs to be acknowledged that the UK scheme is an improvement all round on the circumstances that existed pre-1992 and it does make an important contribution to the accountability of boards, but it would seem that the provision for board accountability has not been achieved to the degree that we are often led to believe; it only really deals with reporting and disclosure and none of the other important stages of accountability, such as explanation and justification, and the consequences of accountability."

Keay Article 'Assessing Accountatility of Boards under UK Corporate Govnernace code,'

Ultimately the issue with the corporate governance code is that it relies on shareholder enforcement and fails to have clear metrics for success. Companies can fail, fail to explain themselves, while this is a consideration for shareholders it often is only insofar as there are already signs of concern.

The trouble is that it gives control to shareholders who then do not use it because the going seems fine. It struggles to proactively prevent corporate scandals. The flexibility means that it isn't taken seriously and the measures that are supposed to be complied with are ignored.

Question 2:
Explain the concept of shareholder stewardship and how it positions shareholders and the exercise of shareholder rights relative to investee companies.

!ANSWER
Shareholder stewardship is the idea that it is the shareholder's duty to examine the long term interests of the company and they act to defend the interest of the company from the board.

Supplementary question:

Pink Jeans PLC is a large UK listed company operating in the fast fashion sector. The company has a new non-executive director (NED), Joan Hunter, recently appointed to the company’s Sustainability Committee. Ms Hunter recently attended the company’s AGM. 

Some investors, at this meeting, complained about a lack of information available concerning the company’s adherence to the principles and provisions of the Corporate Governance Code (2024), which, according to one investor, is 'low on detail’ and ‘fails to clearly state the company’s purpose’ or ‘to report on stakeholder engagement methods.’ 

Ms Hunter has been through the most recently published Corporate Governance Statement for the company and has concerns herself about the low level of ‘application’ and ‘explanation’ that she noticed. Though this was perhaps, she thought, because the company has been doing so well recently, the Directors weren’t expecting investors to be concerned. Ms Hunter has come to you for advice. Comment on:

a. the responsibilities of Pink Jeans PLC relative to the Corporate Governance Code 2024 and whether the situation would differ if the business were a private company. 

b. The connection between financial performance and code compliance, and how directors might be persuaded to tackle the issues identified by investors over the code.
