You have to submit two documents when you decide to make a company you need to submit an article of association and a memorandum. The constitution gives the rules of the company and includes special resolutions. Includes shareholder agreements. Enforceable in contract. S.32 shareholders are given copies of the constitution. A company is supposed to have a constitution. Companies had objects and going beyond that was ultra vires. However as of 2006, they are no longer limited. Any objects recognized previously still have effect. You can modify the articles of association to remove an article. Assume that a company objects are unrestricted.

Articles of association regulate the affairs within a company. There are model articles within the companies act. If they are before that act, then the model articles aren't those from 2008 those are stated in the companies act of 1985 and many companies are still regulated by these articles. These articles give voting proceedures, details of dividens etc. It is a form of power allocation between the board of directors and the general meeting. Shareholders have residual powers a 75% resolution is necessary for it to bind the directors. The directors do the general management. All the powers of the company including contracting and day to day management mean directors have broad powers. This helps seperate ownership and management of a company. 

Directors have power to take decisions against the shareholders. Article 4 of the model articles 75% threshold but they can't do anything about any decisions already done. However under S.168 they have the power to remove directors. S.168 only requires a 50% threshold. Powers ot amend the constitution. Changes to the companies articles require a 75% majority. This cannot be amended to do anything illegal and S.25 you can't extort from the shareholders extra money. 

Articles can be altered through the reform mechanism under S.21, a company cannot fetter its statutory provisions. 

Punt v. Symonds case -> He sets up a company and the company purchases his business and he has the power to appoint or remove directors, there couldn't be changes to the articles that gave this power as part of the establishing articles, they were trying to alter the articles to remove this after his death. A company cannot do away with its power to alter the articles of association as lined up in statute. 

Companies have entrenched articles however, entrenchment provisions have rules around them, when the company is formed, or reformed by agreement of all the shareholders and these alter articles, notify registrar, where these articles are more difficult to amend or with more of a proceedure. 

Imperial Hydropathic Hotel Case -> Directors were supposed to serve terms and retire by rotation. The Shareholders were unhappy and it was held that this was illegal because it contravened the articles of association, even though they had the power, they had to follow the proceedures and order of operations.

Informal ways of altering articles. Where there isn't a vote which might go against the articles

Re Duomatic Case -> Case directors who were sharedholders. For the purposes of director salaries they needed to be passed in the general meeting, they faced liquidation, and the directors just took salaries, and the decision made informally by the shareholders was held to be binding. Higher thresholds all the shareholders have a right to attend and have clear evidence of knowledge of what is being voted on.

There are common law limits.

Allen v. Gold Reefs co. of West Africa -> Put a condition that the power to alter articles may only be done for the benefit of the company as a whole. 

Shuttleworth v. Cox Bros & Co. Maidenhead ltd. -> This test from Allen is a primarily subjective test what did the members belief but there was a baseline objective test.

Greenhlgh v. Arderne Cimeas ltd. -> Controversial case construed the element of the company it's for the benefit of the shareholders and that's what benefit of the company was about. There was also hypothetical shareholder as a role for the court to guess and put themselves in the position of it. 

Citco Banking corp. NV. v. Pussers' ltd. -> Lord Hoffman tried to reign it back in and said it was a subjective test and they should not substitute their judgement for that of the shareholders.

Re Charterhouse Capital ltd. -> Private Equity firm with 16 members, they held the equity and had management powers, over time the members were retiring and there was a split between the members who were active and the old members. They tried to acquire the shares about the older retiring members, one of them wanted to hold onto the shares. What happened was there was an amendment to facilitate compolsory acquisition. Argued that this was not for the benefit of the company as a whole. The threshold was set very high. Even where a member is forced to share, the Allen rule isn't going to save them. Courts are happy to let the majority rule as they wish.

What is the nature of the articles contractarianism set of agreements between the actors in a company. Concessionists view it as a concession and privlege afforded by the state. Contractarians do not believe in state regulation imposed. Concession theorists believe that since the state giveth the state may take away. 

Statutes have binding effect with articles. Statutory contract. The provisions of the contract are binding as lined up in statute. They bind present and future members and the contract.

Hickman v. Kent or Romney Marsh Sheep Breeders Association -> Kept a logbook of sheeps. Dispute between him and the association and he tried to remove himself from the association but the articles of association said that matters had to be resolved by arbitration. The articles of association bind all parties and therefore had to be sent for arbitration. The constitution was signed and sealed by each member, but now recognized in statute that the company is also a signatory to the contract. 

Shareholders cannot sue on behalf of the company the proper way of going about this is that the company itself sues for itself. Shareholders may sue for their own individual rights being violated however. 

Harbottle -> Company as a whole refers to the shareholders of the company present and future. 

MacDougall v. Gardiner -> Courts are reluctant to intervene in the internal affairs of the company. There was a meeting however a number of shareholders sought a vote for certain provisions, but the chairman shot that down. There was a breach of the articles however. 

‘if the thing complained of is a thing which in substance the majority of the company are entitled to do, or if something has been done irregularly which the majority of the company are entitled to do regularly, or if something has been done illegally which the majority of the company are entitled to do legally, there can be no use in having a litigation about it, the ultimate end of which is only that a meeting has to be called, and then ultimately the majority gets its wishes…Of course, if the majority are abusing their powers, and are depriving the minority of their rights, that is an entirely different thing…’

Pender v. Lushington -> Voting rights are property rights. He circumvented a cap on the votes. The courts recognized that enforcing the votes is enforcing property rights. The courts have recognized this. 

Wood v. Odessa Waterworks -> Court upheld shareholders' right to an injection in respect of a decision of the directors to pay dividends in debanture bonds rather than cash. The proposal was inconsistent with the articles of association and the articles alone were what the court had to consider. 

Edwards v. Halliwell -> Rules concerning voting at a union meeting not respected, found to be personal right of members. There was a special reason why members could bring action as they were suing in their own right to protect from invasions of their rights as members. 

Lord Wedderburn Foss v. Harbottle -> Provies a set of rights that come with shareholder ownership, voting rights, rights to have directors appointed in accordance with articles, rights to protect class rights of shareholders rights to enforce a dividend that has been declared share transfer rights pre-emption rights. 

Eley v. Positive Governemnt Security Life Assurance co. -> Solicitor put the articles into the company that he was to be the company's solicitor and when the company stopped using them, he was trying to enforce this article as a solicitor and as a solicitor he was not party to the contract and therefore could not enforce it. 

Beattie v. Beattie -> Paid some stuff to him as his capacity as a director he pointed to an article that said that membership disputes were supposed to be done through arbitration, but he was being sued for his conduct as a director rather than as a member and therefore he could not enforce. 

Quin & Axtens ltd. v. Salmon -> Director's right to veto upheld. Two directors had right to veto some property transactions and they went ahead. The shareholders wanted to go around this veto. The articles of association gave manegerial responsibilities and veto and going around them was going around the articles. Therefore the veto could not be gone around. 

Globalink Telecommunications ltd. v. Wilmbury ltd. -> Indemnity Provision where it was required that the directors be indemnified. It was held not to be valid, as this could be done through the contract rather than in the articles. 

Shareholder Agreements -> These are a common theme binding agreement for shareholders to do or refrain from doing something. Part of the constitution. These agreements might be between all or some of the shareholders without the company as a body and form part of the constitution. 

Kulkani v. Gwent Holdings ltd. -> Mr. Kulkani entered into an agreement, there was a shareholder agreement if one repeatedly breached the shareholder agreement then those shares were transferred to him right away. The breaches had to be gross and there was a 10 day limit to ask for them to be transferred. Notice must be offered and as a result the transfers were not valid. 

Re Compound Photonics Group ltd. -> Tech startup held by investor 80% of shares relations did not work out and the investor sought to push out the original people. However there was a shareholder agreement to act in good faith. The interpretation of good faith. This was held not to be an actionable clause. They could use their statutory powers to act as they wish. 

Puddephat v. Leith -> Shareholder who transferred his shares as security to a lender, as part of the shareholder agreement the votes would be done in line with the wishes of the original person however there was a binding contract to vote in a particular way. So they can regulate how these agreements work. 

Russell v. Northern Bank Development Corp ltd. -> Agreement not to raise more capital. Lord Jauncy the two dimensions of shareholder agreements those who agreed would be bound under a personal obligation that each had undertaken and was seperate from the company's own obligations. Raising capital is statutory powers and taking away those rights through a shareholders agreement would be fettering. They can be severable so some parts may be valid and some parts may be invalid and they may be severable. Enforceable against them but not the company.

Fulham Football Club -> Shareholder agreements can be enforceable against other shareholders, agreement to arbitrate disputes can be enforceable, though there is a statutory remedy to go to court. 

Less likely to be enforceable that preclude rights to wind up company and equitable grounds under insolvency, because of rights and interests of third parties.
