INDOOR MANAGEMENT RULE

Where the articles of association provide for a given action then a third party can safely presume that those actions are complied with. 

(from Turquand, also elaborated in Mahoney v. East Holyford mining co)

"Where there are persons conducting the affairs of the company in a manner which appears to be perfeclty consonant with the articles of association then those wso dealing with them externally are not to be affected by any irregularities which may then take place within the internal management."

However the articles that are publicly avaiable to state a clear limitation on this power with the money threshold, but the third party can presume those elements were complied with. Therefore the transaction could not be stopped.

STATUTORY ROUTE

S.40(1) Companies Act 2006 

In favour of a person dealing with a company in good faith, the power of the directors to bind the company or authorise others to do so shall be deemed to be free of any limitations under the company's constitution. 

Removes limitations imposed by the srticles on the powers of the board to contract for the company. 

Good faith will be presumed so long as there are no indications of the contrary going beyond just money into something more. (Jason Technologies ltd v. Ucominest llc)

Actual authority is conferred through an action of the board or through a position. Managing Director is generally held to be one of those positions.

Apparent authority has 4 elements.

1. Representation: The third party was given a representation that the agent had authoirty to enter into that kind of contract on the company's behalf.

2. Authorised source: The representation was made by someone with actual authority to manage the company's business generally or the matters to which the contract is related.

3. Reliance: The third party relied on that representation when entering into the contract.

4. Representation may arise through conduct.
